HEADLINE: Epstein worked the doors of three continents' cabinets, before office and after, and only the foreign governments have answered for it.
BODY:
<p>Read the cabinets as a ledger and the shape is cultivation. He reached people on their way into office and on their way out: Lutnick before Commerce, Barrack between his posts, Bannon after the White House, Ruemmler two months after she left it, Burns on his last day at State, writing “I remain very much interested in consulting or advisory arrangements”. A man who works the door does not need the office-holder's act. He needs the introduction, and the one after it.</p>
<p>The ones he could not reach, he watched. On the eve of a nomination: “do you know bill barr. CIA .” The senators' questions to the man who signed his deal sat in his own email. Tracked, not met, and kept on file.</p>
<p>Then the consequences, and where they fell. London arrested a former Business Secretary on suspicion of misconduct in public office. Oslo made a former Prime Minister a suspect and had his immunity lifted. Paris opened an investigation into a former Culture Minister. In Washington the Deputy Attorney General's office “would not intervene” in 2008, and the 2025 memo found nothing that “could predicate an investigation against uncharged third parties.” At home the same Department holds the files, reads them and decides whether there is a case. Abroad the prosecutor is not the custodian of the archive. That difference alone could explain the map of who has answered and who has not.</p>
<p>Then the world's richest person, Senior Adviser to the President in 2025. He asked Epstein for the date of the wildest party and said for years that he declined. In June 2025 he posted that the President was in the files, while his own emails sat in them unreleased, and the post came down. What did he know when he wrote it?</p>
<p>Behind the son's ledger stands the father's. A Downing Street file of “Prime Minister's meetings with Robert Maxwell”, Shamir leading the mourners, Pergamon printing the speeches of the Eastern bloc's leaders. Two generations, the same door, opened from the same side.</p>
