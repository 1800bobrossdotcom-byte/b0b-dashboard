## The naming error comes first

- **Teflon is PTFE**, an inert polymer and a brand.
- **PFOA (C8)** is the surfactant DuPont used to *make* fluoropolymers at Washington Works, Parkersburg WV, from 1951.
  EPA: it "is not consumed during the polymerization process".
- **Every human and water measurement read is of PFOA or other PFAS. None is of PTFE.**
- "Teflon in the blood" comes from EWG's 2005 phrase "the Teflon chemical PFOA". It is shorthand for PFOA, and the page
  must say PFOA.
- The documented harm from PTFE itself is a separate thing: **polymer fume fever**, flu-like and usually self-limited,
  from fumes when a pan is overheated.

## The three phrases, graded

### 1. "DuPont poisoning": documented as exposure, concealment and payment; causation only at a settlement standard

- **The 1981 document. EPA's record:**
  - DuPont held a one-page 1981 table of eight pregnant workers, with a column marking each child "normal" or having
    birth defects.
  - It holds cord-blood C8 values for two children: "Normal child – born April 1981. Umbilical cord blood 0.055 ppm."
  - EPA treated it as the first human evidence of PFOA crossing the placenta.
  - **DuPont did not report it.** EPA saw it only in 2001, when the plaintiffs' attorney sent it.
  - DuPont admits the omission from its June 1997 reply to EPA. It called the data "merely confirmatory".
- **The count of birth defects is attributed only:** "two of the seven … eye and nostril defects", in the New York
  Attorney General's complaint of 9 July 2026, an untried allegation. No primary read gives the count.
- **"3M's placental-transfer finding" is not supported.** The 3M exhibit is a 1981 note about *rat* eye defects. The same
  memo walks the finding back as a lab artefact.
- **The 2005 EPA settlement, documented:**
  - eight counts, seven of them under TSCA §8(e);
  - a $10.25m penalty plus $6.25m in projects, which EPA called its largest civil administrative penalty under any
    statute;
  - DuPont "neither admits nor denies the factual allegations";
  - EPA's own theoretical maximum for the 1981 count alone was $183.8m.
- **Leach v. DuPont (2001), documented:**
  - the settlement totalled at least $107.6m;
  - the C8 Health Project took blood from about 69,000 people;
  - the C8 Science Panel found **six "probable links"**: high cholesterol, ulcerative colitis, thyroid disease,
    testicular cancer, kidney cancer and pregnancy-induced hypertension.
  - "Probable link" is a settlement standard, "more likely than not", not a causation finding.
- **The Panel's nulls, at equal weight:** it found no probable link to **birth defects**, miscarriage or stillbirth,
  preterm birth or low birth weight, ADHD or learning disability, type 2 diabetes, stroke, or any cancer other than
  kidney and testicular.
- **The money, all from company filings; every settlement carries no-admission language:**
  - Ohio MDL: verdicts in Bartlett, Freeman and Vigneron (the last with $10.5m in punitive damages); the 2017 settlement
    of $670.7m; about $83m in 2021; $58.5m in 2024; the MDL closed Feb 2025.
  - Public water systems: $1.185bn, final in Feb 2024.
  - 3M's separate water settlement: $10.5–12.5bn, paid through 2036.
  - New Jersey: $875m over 25 years, approved Aug 2026.
  - Ohio $110m and Delaware $50m.
- **Still open:**
  - The federal Chemours consent decree announced 24 June 2026: a $22.5m penalty "based on its ability to pay", with a
    total cost "estimated to exceed $450 million". Entry by the court is not confirmed.
  - North Carolina's case is pending.
  - New York's suit was filed 9 July 2026.
  - AFFF personal-injury cases, about 11,300 of them, have no settlement.
  - Chemours still discloses a DOJ inquiry under the Food, Drug and Cosmetic Act, whether to open a criminal
    investigation, and a 2020 grand-jury subpoena. **No charge is stated.**
- **Corporate denials, printed as theirs:**
  - Chemours "never used" PFOA, and Corteva says EIDP "never made or sold PFOA as a commercial product".
  - Chemours's own 10-K says pre-2015 DuPont made PFOA at Fayetteville. The denials are consistent only on the narrow
    reading "sold as a product".

### 2. "Teflon attaching to DNA": unsupported

- EPA (2016): PFOA "lacks the ability to react with and modify DNA". Its tumours are "likely due to nongenotoxic
  mechanism".
- What PFOA does attach to is **albumin**, a blood protein: 99.7% bound in vitro. That is why its half-life in people is
  about 2.3 years.
- Genotoxicity is mixed:
  - ATSDR: it "can produce DNA damage, but is not mutagenic at noncytotoxic concentrations";
  - ATSDR located no human genotoxicity studies.
- **IARC 2023 put PFOA in Group 1, "carcinogenic to humans", and the basis matters:**
  - sufficient evidence in animals;
  - strong mechanistic evidence in exposed people for **epigenetic alterations** (DNA methylation patterns and miRNAs)
    and **immunosuppression**;
  - only "limited" human evidence for kidney and testicular cancer.
  - IARC itself says the class "does not indicate the level of cancer risk".
  - PFOS is in Group 2B.
- **The defensible version of the author's phrase:** PFOA is associated with changes in how genes are marked
  (methylation). It does not bind DNA.
- Cord-blood methylation studies (Hokkaido, Healthy Start, ECHO, Upstate KIDS) show small, inconsistent associations.
- **The contest, printed:** a 2024 commentary in *Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology* says IARC "overstated" the
  evidence. Its authors' affiliation was not read at source.

### 3. "Children born with Teflon in their systems": documented for PFOA; the name is wrong

- **Documented:**
  - ATSDR: "Some PFAS cross the placenta and enter umbilical cord blood."
  - Baltimore births 2004–05: PFOA in 100% of 299 cord sera, geometric mean 1.6 ng/mL.
  - Breast milk adds a postnatal route: infant PFOA rose "8-11% per week of exclusive breast-feeding" (Sweden).
  - DuPont's own 1981 table already carried cord-blood C8 values.
- **The decline:**
  - US serum PFOA fell from 5.2 to 1.42 ng/mL between 1999–2000 and 2017–18. That is about 73% lower, my arithmetic.
  - It fell after the 2006 PFOA Stewardship Program; the companies *state* they met its goals.
  - Still, "more than 96%" of 12–19-year-olds in 2017–March 2020 had measurable legacy PFAS (CDC).
- **What it does to children, at tier:**
  - Birth weight: Johnson et al. 2014 meta-analysis, −18.9 g per ng/mL. The authors say reverse causation "cannot" be
    disproved.
  - NASEM 2022 rates lower birth weight "sufficient" evidence. **The C8 Panel found no probable link to low birth
    weight.** Contested.
  - Vaccine antibodies: the Faroe cohort found lower levels, a marker. NASEM finds insufficient evidence of more
    infection.
  - **Birth defects: the C8 Panel null.** The 1981 defect count is an allegation only.

## Regulatory status, 24 Oct 2026

- EPA's 2024 drinking-water rule set limits of 4.0 ppt for PFOA and PFOS, with a health goal of zero. GenX, PFHxS and
  PFNA were set at 10 ppt.
- On 20 May 2026 EPA proposed:
  - keeping the PFOA/PFOS limits but allowing exemptions to 2031;
  - rescinding the other four on procedural grounds.
- Comments closed 20 July. **No final rule had been published by 24 Oct.**
- The 2024 rule is in the D.C. Circuit (No. 24-1188). A merits ruling was not located.
