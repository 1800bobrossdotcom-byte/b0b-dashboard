# SUBNATIONAL LIAISON — municipal police running their own foreign intelligence liaison

Internal research (research/ is never served). Supports the **"liaison is ordinary rather
than exotic"** limb of the Section II hypothesis. Compiled 2026-08-31.

**Subject:** the NYPD International Liaison Program (ILP / IDLP / ILU) — a municipal police
department maintaining permanent officers embedded in foreign police services, financed by a
private foundation, with no statute creating it and no legislature voting its budget.

**Scope discipline.** This is about STRUCTURE. Every named officer and official below appears
as the holder of a public office doing a documented job. Nothing here asserts, implies, or
should be read to imply that any living person is a criminal or an intelligence asset. The
finding is about *the arrangement*, not the people in it.

**Tiering used throughout:**
- **[DOCUMENTED]** — primary record, or the institution's own published statement.
- **[ATTRIBUTED]** — reported by a named outlet, sourced to named or unnamed persons; credible, single- or thin-sourced.
- **[NOT ESTABLISHED]** — looked for, did not find. Stated as a fact about *the search*, not about the world (per DATED-NULLS standing method note).

## 0. THE FINDING IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The largest municipal police department in the United States has run a permanent overseas
intelligence-liaison network for roughly two decades — currently 14 foreign posts plus
domestic ones — in which detectives are embedded inside foreign national police services.
The officers' **salaries** are public money in the NYPD budget. Their **travel, housing, rent
and office costs** are paid by a private 501(c)(3), the New York City Police Foundation. No
statute, local law, or City Council resolution was found that creates or authorizes the
program; the NYPD's own public description of it cites no legal authority. Until 2024 no law
required the private side of the funding to be disclosed at all. The City Comptroller has
never audited the Intelligence Division. The NYPD Inspector General, whose jurisdiction on
its face covers the program, has never published a report on it, and by August 2025 had three
to five filled positions. The NYPD itself, writing in the journal of the International
Association of Chiefs of Police, describes these liaisons as **"unique among major municipal
police agencies in the United States."** That last sentence is the base rate, and it is the
finding: this is not ordinary municipal practice replicated across cities — it is one city.

## 3. FUNDING AND OVERSIGHT STRUCTURE

### 3.1 The split
The defining structural fact, and it is documented from two independent directions:

> **"Although these officers' salaries are paid by the Department, all other expenses are
> covered by the privately funded and managed New York City Police Foundation. The budget for
> the overseas program is not public…"** — Brennan Center, 2012 [DOCUMENTED]

Corroborated by ABC7 2024 ("the nonprofit Police Foundation covers travel and housing costs"),
by NYCPF's own page, and by DNAinfo 2014 ("room and board and supplies").

So: **the officers are on the public payroll; the foreign presence is privately financed.**
The City Council appropriates the Intelligence Bureau's personnel budget in the ordinary
course. What it has never appropriated — because the money never enters the city treasury —
is the cost of *stationing those officers abroad*.

### 3.2 The Foundation
- **New York City Police Foundation, Inc.**, EIN 13-2711338. Founded **1971** by the
  Association for a Better New York. 501(c)(3). [DOCUMENTED]
- NYCPF's own current description: **"For more than 20 years, the Foundation has supported the
  NYPD's International/Domestic Liaison Program (IDLP), placing Senior Intelligence Officers
  in major cities around the world to gather and share critical intelligence on terrorism,
  transnational crime, and emerging threats."** [DOCUMENTED]
- **FY2024: IDLP = $1,204,125**, the largest program line in the first Local Law 25 report.
  [DOCUMENTED, but see §7 — the PDF is Akamai-blocked from this environment]
- Historic scale: "several hundred thousand dollars… annually" for the overseas deployment
  (WNYC 2011); "more than $1 million a year" (DNAinfo 2014). [ATTRIBUTED]
- ProPublica (2014) found the Foundation had distributed **over $120 million in grants since
  its creation without any government oversight**. [ATTRIBUTED, via Wikipedia's summary]

### 3.3 Kelly's own framing of why it is private
> **"Our overseas program, our counter-terrorism program…studies by the Rand

That is the commissioner of police saying, on the record, that the point of the private

## 4. LEGAL AUTHORITY — THE CENTRAL NULL

**This is the item the brief flagged as only publishable if actually searched. It was
searched. Here is exactly what was and was not found.**

**[NOT ESTABLISHED]** — No statute, local law, City Council resolution, executive order, or
published memorandum of understanding creating or authorizing the International Liaison
Program was located. Searches covered: NYC Charter text, NYC Administrative Code, NYC Council
Legistar, intro.nyc local-law indexes, the NYPD's own published pages, the NYPD Patrol Guide
rules published on nycrules, DOI/OIG-NYPD authorizing materials, the Brennan Center's legal
survey, and an international-law review treatment of the program (IELR, 2018) which
**expressly discusses no statutory authority, no MOU, and no host-nation consent mechanism**
and "provides no primary legal documents establishing the ILP's authority."

**What exists instead, positively:**

1. **Commissioner's general Charter power.** NYC Charter **§434** ("Commissioner; powers and
   duties") vests the Commissioner with cognizance and control of the government,
   administration, disposition and discipline of the Department; §434(b) with §1043 is the
   Department's rulemaking authority. This is the residual authority under which the ILP most
   plausibly sits. **[ATTRIBUTED]** — I could not retrieve §434's verbatim text (both
   amlegal and Justia returned 403 from this environment; see §7), so the *characterization*
   is sourced, the *quotation* is not. **Nothing found in any source says §434 or any other
   Charter provision mentions stationing officers outside the city, foreign operations, or
   intelligence liaison.**

2. **Host-nation invitation, asserted but not documented.** Deputy Commissioner Paul Browne,
   April 2013: the placements occurred **"at the invitation of the foreign countries
   involved."** [ATTRIBUTED] No invitation, agreement, exchange of notes, or status
   arrangement has been published for any post.

3. **Strong negative corroboration that no status agreement exists.** The 2024 tax collapse is
   the best evidence available that the posts rest on nothing formal: three posts (Toronto,
   Montreal, Singapore) closed because officers became **personally** liable for host-country
   taxes, the NYPD having never established tax or status agreements with host nations. A
   retired detective reports paying half a million dollars out of pocket. [ATTRIBUTED — NY
   Post, 25 May 2024.] A program operating under a treaty, a SOFA-type instrument, or even a
   documented inter-agency MOU would not fail this way. This corroborates Giraldi's 2012
   claim that the officers have "no legal standing for dealing with the local authorities."

**Publishable formulation:** *No statute creates the ILP, no legislature votes its budget, and
no published instrument governs the officers' status abroad. It exists at commissioner
discretion under the Police Commissioner's general Charter powers, is financed for its
extraterritorial component by a private foundation, and rests abroad on invitation rather than
on any documented agreement — a proposition corroborated by the program's own 2024 failure to
survive contact with host-country tax law.*

That formulation is defensible. Do **not** write "there is no legal authority" flatly — write
that none was found and that the NYPD itself has never cited one.

## 5. BASE RATE — DO OTHER US CITIES DO THIS?

**This is the load-bearing comparison, and it resolves against "ordinary."**

**[DOCUMENTED] The NYPD says it is alone.** The strongest evidence is the NYPD's own, written
by its own intelligence leadership in the journal of the International Association of Chiefs
of Police:

> "…the Intelligence Bureau's International Liaison Unit (ILU), which includes 14 overseas and
> 3 domestic liaison posts that are embedded in local law enforcement or international policing
> agencies. **These specialized liaisons, unique among major municipal police agencies in the
> United States**, provide firsthand daily reporting to NYPD decision makers…"
> — John Tully Gordon & Rebecca Ulam Weiner, "Light Pierces Through," *Police Chief* 88:9
> (Sept 2021), pp. 34–53

**LAPD** — [DOCUMENTED] The next-largest municipal counterterrorism operation in the country
(300 officers vs the NYPD's ~1,000 across CT + Intel, per Brennan 2012). In **July 2007**,
Chief William Bratton was **"trying to get several cities to pool resources to station
detectives overseas, as New York has for several years"** (Judith Miller, Manhattan Institute,
15 Jul 2007) — a plan under which liaison officers would share reports with the departments
that helped finance the posts. **[NOT ESTABLISHED]** that this was ever implemented; no
evidence of an LAPD foreign post was found, and the 2021 NYPD statement above implies it was
not. Note the inversion on oversight: the **LAPD's Inspector General audits the LAPD
Intelligence Unit annually**, reviewing whether the reasonable-suspicion threshold was met and
whether surveillance was properly authorized, with public versions of the reports released

**Chicago** — **[NOT ESTABLISHED]**. Repeated searches returned nothing indicating CPD

**Everyone else** — Every comparable US overseas law-enforcement presence found is

**Conclusion for Section II.** The base rate is approximately **one**. Foreign intelligence
liaison by a US sub-national police force is not a widespread municipal practice that the NYPD
happens to participate in; it is a practice the NYPD invented and, by its own account, still
holds alone. This *sharpens* rather than weakens the "liaison is ordinary" limb — but it must
be argued the right way. The ordinariness is not "lots of cities do it." The ordinariness is
that **a liaison relationship of this kind can be stood up by an administrative decision, run
for two decades, financed privately, and never require a statute, an appropriation, a treaty,
or an audit.** The exotic thing about foreign intelligence liaison turns out not to be the
liaison. It is the paperwork nobody needed.

## 6. THE DEMOGRAPHICS UNIT — KEPT SEPARATE

**These are two different programs and the report must not merge them.** They share a parent
(the NYPD Intelligence Division), an era (post-9/11), a patron (Commissioner Kelly), and a
CIA connection — but they are distinct in mandate, geography, personnel and fate.

| | **International Liaison Program** | **Demographics Unit** |
|---|---|---|
| Mandate | Officers embedded in *foreign* police services; liaison and reporting | Mapping and monitoring *domestic* Muslim communities in NY/NJ |
| Geography | 14 foreign + 3 domestic posts | New York City and surrounding states |
| CIA link | David Cohen, former CIA DDO, as Deputy Commissioner of Intelligence (an NYPD official, appointed) | Lawrence Sanchez, an active CIA officer seconded to the NYPD **while on the CIA's payroll**, "instrumental in creating" the unit |
| Size | ~18 officers (2025) | 16 officers speaking ≥5 languages |
| Funding | Salaries public; overseas costs by NYCPF | Not established that NYCPF funded it |
| Status | **Active and expanding** (Accra opening 2026) | **Dismantled 15 Apr 2014** under Mayor de Blasio |
| Public record | Acknowledged by NYPD on its own website | NYPD **denied the unit existed** until AP published the documents |

**The AP reporting.** Matt Apuzzo, Adam Goldman, Eileen Sullivan and Chris Hawley of the
**Associated Press** won the **2012 Pulitzer Prize for Investigative Reporting** for the
series exposing the NYPD's clandestine surveillance of Muslim communities; the Pulitzer board
noted it produced "congressional calls for a federal investigation, and a debate over the
proper role of domestic intelligence gathering." [DOCUMENTED]

**The only legitimate connection to draw** is the oversight one, and it is a real one: the AP
series is the source of the finding that **the Comptroller had never audited the Intelligence
Division**, and the outrage it generated produced **Local Law 70 of 2013** and the OIG-NYPD —
an oversight body that, twelve years on, has still published nothing on the ILP and is down to
three to five staff. The Demographics Unit is how New York learned that its Intelligence
Division was unaudited. The ILP is what remained unaudited afterward.
