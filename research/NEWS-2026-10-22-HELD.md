1. **New Mexico sues the Epstein estate, its 1953 Trust and the estate's two co-executors (D.N.M. 1:26-cv-03543,
   21 Oct).**
   - The claims are under the federal Trafficking Victims Protection Act and the New Mexico Racketeering Act. The
     State asks the court to block estate payments to the co-executors.
   - **Documented:** the filing, the court, the parties and the relief sought. **Untested:** every allegation in it
     is the State's. The co-executors have told Congress they did not know of the abuse.
   - **The complaint was not opened.** It is built partly on protective-order and survivor-identifying material
     (stop rule b).
   - **Why held:** the State's wording ("knowingly participated", "concealing") about living, uncharged people is
     defamation-adjacent. The page already names both men as co-executors in II and IV, so whether the new
     allegation sits beside their names is the author's decision.
   - **If published:** one line in the XV status or II, "New Mexico has sued …, alleging …; the allegations are the
     State's and untested", with the co-executors' answer to Congress beside it.
2. **PHP2Shell (CVE-2026-109618 / -109619): exploitation status and a disputed release.**
   - CISA still reports no observed exploitation, and the CVEs are not in the KEV catalogue (version 2026.10.21,
     read 22 Oct).
   - Amazon: "PHP2Shell is now being broadly exploited in the wild", with an attempt dated 17 Oct, four days before
     disclosure. Amazon cannot say any attempt would have succeeded. Germany's BSI, the Dutch NCSC and Japan's
     government report attempts but no confirmed successful use.
   - The release itself is in dispute: the PHP Foundation says Chainguard broke the embargo by about six hours, and
     Chainguard's chief executive says it shipped because people were already being hit.
   - **Our exposure is unchanged** from the 21 Oct check (CLAUDE.md §7): this repo and `cbay` hold no PHP.
   - **Why held:** the 21 Oct rule was to note it in a sweep only if exploitation is reported, and it now is, by
     Amazon, against CISA's silence. **The Anthropic disclosure applies:** the PHP Foundation says it scanned PHP
     with Anthropic's model and thanks Anthropic for tool access and funding.
3. **Anthropic v. Department of War (D.C. Cir. 26-1049 / 26-1162).**
   - The panel ruled 2–1 for the Department on 25 Sept, with one judge dissenting. The majority says the exclusion
     followed Anthropic's refusal "to relax contractual prohibitions on the use of Claude for lethal autonomous
     warfare or domestic surveillance".
   - Anthropic petitioned for panel rehearing and rehearing en banc on 13 Oct. Amicus briefs were filed on 20–21 Oct.
     The Department has asked to answer by 13 Nov.
   - **Why held:** Anthropic is the petitioner, and the report is drafted with Anthropic's model. If carried, the
     standing disclosure goes in the same paragraph.
4. **ProPublica (via Defense One), 19 Oct: DHS political appointees asked CISA to acquire and test a voting machine
   in secret.**
   - The sources are two anonymous former officials. The White House defended its record; DHS and CISA did not
     answer.
   - It is out of window (the 20 Oct sweep missed it). **Why held:** anonymous sourcing about named offices.
5. **Amnesty International on Morocco's DGST (1 Oct): spyware against a human-rights defender.**
   - **Why held:** out of window. The target is named in the source (a victim), and the report has not been read in
     full here.
6. **eMudhra (19–22 Oct): weak CA keys and its exit from TLS.**
   - Cloudflare says private keys for eight eMudhra intermediate certificates were "compromised". Because the keys
     are weak, a certificate could be forged for any domain. Cloudflare found no active exploitation on its own
     services.
   - eMudhra says a flaw in its 30 Apr 2025 key ceremony produced weak keys, and that an independent researcher
     found they could be reverse-engineered, "possibly with AI-assisted techniques". It says its infrastructure was
     not compromised.
   - On 22 Oct eMudhra's board withdrew from the global TLS certificate business.
   - The browser root programmes' own statements were not read.
   - **Why held:** documented, but its relevance to the report is marginal.
7. **France: the riots since 17 Oct and the Alliance4Europe finding.**
   - Documented: the justice minister says 2,700 people were taken into custody and 217 jailed after fast-track
     trials (France 24, 22 Oct). There are curfews in parts of Paris and protest bans in Marseille and Lyon.
   - Attributed: research by the NGO Alliance4Europe "suggests that pro-Russian networks manipulated information
     online" to exacerbate the anger, tracing a campaign back to early September. No Russian response was found.
   - **Why held:** no state operator is established, and the influence claim rests on one NGO. If carried, it goes
     in as the NGO's finding, beside the custody figures. It was left out of the status for length.
8. **An exiled Rwandan ex-soldier and critic of the government found beheaded near Kampala (France 24, 20 Oct).**

   - **Why held:** one caption, and no written report or official account. The victim is not named here.
