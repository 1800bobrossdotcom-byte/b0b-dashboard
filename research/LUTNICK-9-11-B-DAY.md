## 3. eSpeed and the Treasury market reopening, 13 Sept 2001

The government bond market was closed on 11 and 12 September and reopened on 13 September, as eSpeed's own 10-Q
states. eSpeed reconnected "47 hours after the attacks", in time for the reopening, running from data centres in
Rochelle Park (NJ) and London. Those centres are documented in the 10-K filed March 2001, six months before the
attack. Cantor's voice brokerage did not come back.

The independent official record is GAO-03-251 (Feb 2003). It names Cantor as the most notable of the eight of nine
interdealer brokers (IDBs) whose operations were severely disrupted, and says that day's trades had to be rebuilt from
the dealers' own records. The NY Fed's settlement-fails study ties the fails spike to destroyed brokers' offices and
records. Neither source names an act by Cantor or Lutnick beyond the disruption.

Nasdaq halted eSpeed's stock at the company's request on 17 Sept; trading resumed on 5 Oct ("tomorrow", 4 Oct
release). The Q3 10-Q was filed late (NT 10-Q, 13 Nov), "Due to the effects on the Registrant of the destruction of"
its offices.

## 4. The pay stoppage and the relief pledge, dated

- **Sept 15 (Sat):** the last paychecks for the missing went out, covering through 30 Sept. Bloomberg (2002) dates the
  stoppage "four days after". Lutnick's framing is that salaries were paid on the 15th and not continued past the 30th.
- **Sept 19:** his first public statement of the 25% figure is on Larry King, taped that afternoon. The Washington
  Examiner (2026) dates the announcement to 19 Sept. The same broadcast read out the Relief Fund's contact address.
- **Oct 10:** the detailed package (Newsday, 11 Oct): ten years of health cover, 25% of profits for five years, 2001
  bonuses, and life-insurance payments. Profits would not be drawn from eSpeed.
- **Oct 25:** Cantor began distributing $45 million in bonuses to families.
- **Feb 2002:** the first cash profit-share payments.
- **2006:** $180 million paid in total, closing the pledge (Wikipedia; also Fox/AP).

The Relief Fund was "established immediately after the attacks" and had paid $8 million by late December 2001; New
York magazine (10 Dec) says $8.5 million.

## 5. The Convar hard-drive story (Dec 2001)

request). Convar, a German data-recovery firm, said it had recovered data from 32 WTC-area computers showing an
unexplained surge in transactions, chiefly credit-card transactions, possibly over $100 million. Its clients were
"credit card companies, telecommunications firms and accountants". It named no client, citing client privacy, and its
director said legitimate reasons were possible.

reports give the Convar data expert two different first names and two different roles (WITHHELD).
