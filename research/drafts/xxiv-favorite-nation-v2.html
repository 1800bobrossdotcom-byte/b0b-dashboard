A favorite nation - Washington’s test, run against the US–Israel record

The claim put to this section, in the author’s words, is that the relationship between the United States and Israel is an unhealthy one - a foreign power’s influence operating inside American policy - and that it reaches as far as a coup, or foreign control of the United States. He has since asked for the record to be run from 1960, “practically and logistically”, starting with the attack on an American ship. The first half is a reading of a record, and the record is large. The second half is a claim about a transfer of power, and it needs a document of its own. They are graded separately, at the end of the entry that follows this one, because running them together is how the documented half gets thrown out with the undocumented one.

The test is older than either country’s intelligence services, and it is the first President’s. Washington’s Farewell Address, 1796: “Against the insidious wiles of foreign influence (I conjure you to believe me, fellow-citizens) the jealousy of a free people ought to be constantly awake, since history and experience prove that foreign influence is one of the most baneful foes of republican government.” And, on the specific failure: “a passionate attachment of one nation for another produces a variety of evils. Sympathy for the favorite nation, facilitating the illusion of an imaginary common interest in cases where no real common interest exists, and infusing into one the enmities of the other, betrays the former into a participation in the quarrels and wars of the latter without adequate inducement or justification. It leads also to concessions to the favorite nation of privileges denied to others…” Eisenhower’s farewell of 1961 is the domestic twin: “we must guard against the acquisition of unwarranted influence, whether sought or unsought, by the military-industrial complex.” Washington names three things to measure - concessions, wars, and influence - and the record can be run against each. The same address sets the discipline for running it: “that jealousy to be useful must be impartial; else it becomes the instrument of the very influence to be avoided… Excessive partiality for one foreign nation and excessive dislike of another cause those whom they actuate to see danger only on one side.” It names no country, and it binds whoever applies it.

Concessions of privileges denied to others - documented, in statute. Every certification of a US arms sale “to any country in the Middle East other than Israel shall include a determination that the sale… will not adversely affect Israel’s qualitative military edge over military threats to Israel” (22 U.S.C. §2776(h), 2008). That is a binding condition on every other state’s purchases, not a supply guarantee - and it is precisely a privilege denied to others. The declared policy sits beside it: “It is the policy of the United States… To help the Government of Israel preserve its qualitative military edge… To veto any one-sided anti-Israel resolutions at the United Nations Security Council” (22 U.S.C. §8602, 2012). The money is fixed by memorandum: $38 billion for FY2019–2028 ($33bn in Foreign Military Financing, $5bn missile defence), with Israel’s financing paid as a lump sum early in the fiscal year into an interest-bearing account at the Federal Reserve; cumulative US aid stands at $174 billion, and the April 2024 supplemental added $3.5bn in financing, $4bn for Iron Dome and David’s Sling and $1.2bn for Iron Beam (CRS). The human-rights vetting law applied to it differently in practice: in 2024 the State Department found five Israeli units had committed gross violations of human rights and deemed four “remediated”, the fifth following in August, with no unit cut off (attributed, ProPublica and CNN; State’s position is that the same standards apply to every country). And the newest privilege is protection from a court. When the International Criminal Court issued its warrant for Israel’s prime minister (Section XV), the United States answered with sanctions: Executive Order 14203 of February 2025 calls them “baseless arrest warrants targeting Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu”; Treasury has since listed all three judges of the chamber that issued them, and on 9 October 2026 designated the International Criminal Court itself (OFAC). The warrant stands: the Chamber twice declined to withdraw it in 2025 while Israel’s jurisdictional challenge, returned to it by the Appeals Chamber in April 2025, remains undecided. It is a warrant on reasonable grounds; there has been no trial.

The same concession pays the other side of the peace. Egypt has received about $1.3 billion a year in military financing for decades under the framework that followed Camp David - for FY2026 Congress gave $1.375 billion, “an amount $75 million above what Egypt had been receiving as an FMF baseline for decades” (CRS, February 2026) - and the edge requirement above governs every major sale to it, because §2776(h) reaches “any country in the Middle East other than Israel.” The state that money arms built the Octagon and the New Capital (Section X). It also holds a large population of political detainees: estimates run from about 65,000 (Arabic Network for Human Rights Information, March 2021) to 10,000–20,000 (2024) - attributed; Egypt publishes no total, and its president told CBS in January 2019, “We don’t have political prisoners.” Congress conditions $320m of the aid on human rights each year; $750m in total was reprogrammed or withheld across FY2020–FY2023, and in September 2024 the full amount was released, by waiver on $225m and certification on $95m (CRS). For decades the flagship of that system, Tora prison and its maximum-security “Scorpion” wing, stood about two kilometres from the Tura quarries in Section X, in the same district; it was emptied in 2021–22 for new complexes at Badr and Wadi el-Natrun, and an official decision of September 2025 proposes the old site for housing. Egypt does not hold the most political prisoners in the world - North Korea (80,000–120,000, UN Commission of Inquiry, 2014) and Xinjiang at its peak hold more - and this report does not say it does.

And the newest concession in law is pending, not enacted. Section 219 of H.R. 8800, the House’s FY2027 defence authorisation, would require the Secretary of Defense to “designate an executive agent… responsible for synchronizing cooperative efforts between the United States and Israel” - identifying “jointly developed or Israeli-origin technologies… for potential integration into United States systems and programs of record” and establishing “joint ventures, licensing agreements, and United States-based co-production or manufacturing partnerships with Israeli industry.” It carries no dollar figure and no export-control exemption (it requires consistency with existing law). The House passed the bill 216–212 on 22 July 2026, under a rule that did not allow a vote on an amendment to strike §219; the same bill removes the annual caps on US–Israel counter-drone and anti-tunnel cooperation, which the Senate version instead raises to $100m each. The Senate’s counterpart, §1217 of S. 4784, adds consultation with Israel’s Minister of Defense and asks for authorities to promote “the long-term integration of joint capabilities.” Neither is law as this is written. The author’s reading, carried as his: if enacted, this binds US defence research and industry to a foreign state’s interest - a betrayal of the national interest in Washington’s sense of the word. The legal limit on that reading is stated with it: treason under Article III is confined to levying war against the United States or adhering to its enemies, and a statute benefiting an ally cannot meet that definition. The charge is political and constitutional-tradition, not criminal, and it is argued with votes.

Participation in the quarrels and wars of the favorite nation - documented, in the President’s own letter. “On February 28, 2026, the United States and Israel launched military operations against targets in Iran” (Congressional Research Service). The President’s War Powers report of 2 March cited no authorisation from Congress and no finding of imminence; it rested on Article II and listed, among the purposes, “collective self-defense of our regional allies, including Israel.” Israeli and US forces “coordinated closely during active hostilities” (CRS). That the Israeli government pressed for the war over months, including a Situation Room presentation on 11 February, is attributed (New York Times); that Israel at points delayed or was told not to strike is also reported and is carried with it. The cost is documented: 18 Americans killed and 824 wounded as of 15 September, tens of billions spent, and a UN finding against the United States already on this page (Section XV). By 18 September the House had voted six times on resolutions to remove US forces, passing three, and the Senate had voted on eleven, passing one; on 23 June the Senate also agreed to the first House measure, so one passed both chambers - as a concurrent resolution, the form whose binding force has been doubted since INS v. Chadha (1983) (CRS). The attachment also runs the other way. On 18 October 2026, nine days before Israel’s election, the President published “my Complete and Total Endorsement of Bibi Netanyahu in his upcoming Race for Re-Election”, adding “If Israel has the wrong Leader, which would be anyone other than Bibi, America will have to do this work ourselves”; in November 2025 he had written to Israel’s president, “I hereby call on you to fully pardon Benjamin Netanyahu”, who is on trial in Israel for corruption (attributed: the post refused this report’s crawler, and both texts are read as the press quoted them). That is a US President acting in another country’s election and courts - Washington’s passionate attachment, documented from the American side. The record since 1960 - the ship, the reactor, the agents and the registered campaigns

Washington’s third measure is influence. The author asked for it run from 1960, beginning with the ship; this entry does that, channel by channel, sets the result against what other states do, and then grades the whole question - the documented half and the half about control - at its end.

The ship - 8 June 1967. The attack is documented; the intent is contested, and the US record is divided inside itself. Israeli aircraft and torpedo boats attacked the USS Liberty, a US Navy intelligence ship, during the Six-Day War, “in international waters, a little more than 13 nautical miles from the Sinai coast” (FRUS, from the Navy court’s record). Thirty-four Americans were killed. Israel paid $3,323,500 in 1968 for the dead, $3,566,457 in 1969 on 164 injury claims, and $6 million in 1980 for the ship. The United States’ note of 10 June called the attack “quite literally incomprehensible” and “military recklessness reflecting wanton disregard for human life”. Clark Clifford’s report to the President of 18 July cuts both ways: the information “does not reflect that the Israeli high command made a premeditated attack on a ship known to be American”, yet the mistaken-identity account “is unbelievable”, and the attack was “a flagrant act of gross negligence”. The CIA judged it “not made in malice toward the US and was by mistake, but … indicative of gross negligence” (1967), and “gross stupidity and negligence but not malicious intent” (1977). The Navy’s Court of Inquiry was not asked the question: “It was not the responsibility of the Court to rule on the culpability of the attackers, and no evidence was heard from the attacking nation”; its members, by Cyrus Vance’s account, believed in a failure of Israeli communications “and not from premeditation. But in his view, on the evidence we have, we should not so state.” Israel’s own inquiry called it an “innocent mistake”. Against all of that, inside the same government: NSA’s Deputy Director, Louis Tordella, wrote on Israel’s report “A nice whitewash for a group of ignorant, stupid and inept” - and in 2004 the court’s counsel, Captain Ward Boston, swore that “Both Admiral Kidd and I believed with certainty that this attack… was a deliberate effort to sink an American ship and murder its entire crew” and that the court was directed to find mistaken identity (testimony); a private 2003 commission under Admiral Moorer reached the same view (attributed). So the tier is: attack, dead and compensation documented; the official verdicts of both governments say not premeditated; deliberate intent is held by a senior NSA official, the court’s counsel and the survivors, and is not established. In August 2026 the Ninth Circuit held that a withheld House Appropriations report on the attack is a congressional record outside the Freedom of Information Act; it stays withheld.

The 1960s file - three things the United States knew about and did not resolve. Dimona: in 1963 Kennedy pressed for twice-yearly visits with access to “any… plutonium separation plant”; Israel’s terms would “limit us to examination of the reactor alone”, in the US reading of them (FRUS) - documented; that the visits that followed were defeated by concealment is the National Security Archive historians’ finding from Israeli minutes - “None of the nine AEC teams… were ever aware of the super-secret underground facility” (attributed). The American Zionist Council: on 21 November 1962 the Justice Department wrote that funding from the Jewish Agency “constitutes the Council an agent of a foreign principal” and requested registration; a Jewish Agency officer, testifying from recollection to the Senate Foreign Relations Committee in May 1963, put its payments to the Council before 1960 at “around $600,000 to $700,000” a year; by 1965 the Department called it a “bona fide dispute”, accepted a filing that “does not comprise a registration statement”, and the Council never registered (documented, DOJ letters and sworn testimony). NUMEC: the Apollo, Pennsylvania plant could not account for 93.8 kg of enriched uranium by 1966 (AEC) and 368 kg net by 1982 (NRC); a senior CIA official “thought a diversion had taken place” (NRC record, 1978); the FBI dropped its inquiry in 1969 when the AEC took no action. Against it, documented: “no evidence - despite an intensive search for some - to prove that one did” (Brzezinski to the President, 1977), and every investigation “ended with no definitive answer” (GAO, 1978). Diversion to Israel is not established.

The insidious wiles of foreign influence - espionage, documented and some of it adjudicated. Jonathan Pollard, arrested 21 November 1985 while passing classified material to an Israeli intelligence unit, pleaded guilty and was sentenced to life; Israel called it a rogue operation until 1998, when it acknowledged him as an agent “handled by senior members of the Scientific Liaison Bureau”. The US damage assessment of 1987 records, on Pollard’s account, that his taskings were Arab, Pakistani and Soviet - “The Israelis never expressed interest in US military activities, plans, capabilities, or equipment” - and records that his handler also asked for “any information that would identify Israeli officials who were providing information to the United States, and any information on US intelligence operations targeted against Israel”. Ben-Ami Kadish, a former Army employee at Picatinny Arsenal, pleaded guilty in 2008 to conspiring to act as “an unregistered agent of the Government of Israel” for passing documents from about 1980 to 1985, some on US missile defence. A Pentagon analyst, Lawrence Franklin, pleaded guilty in 2005 to passing classified information, including to two lobby employees; the government moved to dismiss their own case in 2009, citing the court’s added intent requirements and “the inevitable disclosure of classified information”, and they were not tried. In 1996 the General Accounting Office reported that an unnamed “Country A conducts the most aggressive espionage operation against the United States of any U.S. ally”; that Country A is Israel is press identification, not GAO’s (attributed). The FBI concluded in 2019 that Israel likely placed phone-surveillance devices near the White House (attributed, Politico; Israel called it a “blatant lie”). The Commerce Department put NSO Group on its Entity List in November 2021 for supplying spyware that governments used “to maliciously target government officials, journalists”, and it remains there; a US court found NSO liable in December 2024 for using WhatsApp’s servers to reach users’ phones, and enjoined it in October 2025; the FBI itself held a Pegasus licence from December 2018 (NSO’s court filing), in the Bureau’s words “for product testing and evaluation only”. And the signals relationship runs one way in a way the others do not: an NSA memorandum published in 2013 shows raw, unminimised intercepts - Americans’ communications included - shared with Israeli intelligence.

Influence operations and money - covert once, registered now, and large. In 2024 Meta removed a network of 510 Facebook accounts, with Pages, a Group and Instagram accounts, aimed at audiences in the United States and Canada including US lawmakers, and linked it to a Tel Aviv political-marketing firm; that Israel’s Diaspora Ministry commissioned it for about $2m is attributed (New York Times), and the ministry denied it. Since September 2025 Israel’s foreign ministry has also paid for influence work in the open, registered under the Foreign Agents Registration Act. Clock Tower X LLC registered work done through Havas Media for the State of Israel’s foreign ministry: a “nationwide campaign in the United states to combat antisemitism”, “At least 80 percent of content… tailored to Gen Z audiences”, and “Deployment of websites and content to deliver GPT framing results on GPT conversations” - content written to shape what AI chatbots answer. Its budget rose from $6m to $9m, it reported $15 million received in the six months to March 2026, and its schedule runs $4.5m a month through October 2026. Its sites carry the legally required notice - “distributed by Clock Tower X LLC on behalf of the State of Israel” - in their footers, under names that do not say so. Another registrant filed a $900,000 influencer order titled “Esther Project” (it reports receiving about $200,000 and says it created no content), and a third a $3.26m invoice for “Christian outreach” in the western United States - a mobile museum for churches and Christian colleges, and online targeting by geofencing. All of it is disclosed and lawful; that is the point of FARA, and the filings are the documents. The money from American citizens is a different instrument: AIPAC is registered as a domestic lobby, not a foreign agent, and appears in no FARA record; its super PAC raised $87.2m in the 2024 cycle and $109.7m in the 2026 cycle to 31 August (FEC), its heaviest spending going against a New York congressman in the 2024 primaries (about $9.9m) and a Michigan Senate candidate in 2026 (about $20.4m) (this report’s sum of FEC filings). How members vote is on the roll: in April 2026, 40 senators backed at least one of two resolutions to block arms sales to Israel, and 59 and 63 voted them down. A vote is a recorded act. This report prints the votes and claims nothing about any member’s loyalty.

The symmetry rule, run on the whole record. Israel is not alone in any of these channels, and on some it is smaller than its neighbours. Under the same statutes the United States has convicted agents of Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Qatar, China and Russia, and acquitted an adviser accused of acting for the UAE; the three adjudicated cases involving Israel sit inside that range, not outside it. In the second half of 2024, FARA filings reported Saudi Arabia’s US agents receiving $31.6m, Japan’s $29.3m, China’s $27.1m, South Korea’s $20.8m and the UAE’s $20.2m, against Israel’s $11.1m, most of that the Jewish Agency and the World Zionist Organization (this report’s sum of the Justice Department’s semi-annual report) - before the 2025–26 contracts above. Criminal FARA cases are rare for everyone: seven between 1966 and 2015 (Justice Department Inspector General). The intelligence community’s annual threat assessments for 2024–2026 name China, Russia, Iran and North Korea for espionage and influence, and do not name Israel as either; its counter-intelligence centre writes, naming no one, that “U.S. allies or partners often take advantage of the access they enjoy to collect sensitive military and civilian technologies”. That silence is evidence about the threat documents, not proof that nothing happens - the guaranteed-null rule, both ways. And one 1985 episode on the usual list is not interference at all: Israel’s arms shipments to Iran were made “with U.S. approval” (Walsh report) - joint conduct, authorised by the President.

Measured against Washington’s test, the record is not soft. A statute conditions every regional arms sale on one country’s advantage; the same framework pays the other side of the peace, to a state that jails its opposition; a policy of the United States commits its Security Council veto; sanctions now shield that country’s leader from an international court; a war was begun jointly, with that country’s defence named in the legal basis; an attack that killed thirty-four Americans was settled for compensation, with its intent never resolved and the report on it still withheld; the United States let a nuclear-material loss and a foreign-agent order lapse unresolved; one of its intelligence services ran an American spy to a life sentence and asked him to identify its own officials who were talking to the United States; its foreign ministry pays for content meant to shape what Americans read and what their chatbots answer; and a pending bill would build its industry into American programs of record. That is the documented half, and on the page it stands at documented tier. The author’s reading - that this is the passionate attachment Washington warned against, and an unhealthy relationship - is carried at the author’s hypothesis tier, and the record gives it a great deal to stand on.

The other half is graded where it stands. “Coup”, “foreign control of the United States”, and “infiltrated” read as control: unsupported at any tier. Nothing read for this entry shows Israeli agents attempting to seize, direct or control any function of the US government. What the record holds is espionage, covert and registered influence operations, lobbying and money, and asymmetries written into American law - channels of influence, every one of them documented, and none of them a transfer of control. The guaranteed-null rule applies in both directions: the absence of a coup record is not evidence of hidden control, and it is not evidence of innocence either; it is evidence that the claim has no document. Rung 5 - tasked and run - is claimed for no one, here as everywhere. And the Section VII guardrail is invoked by name, because this is the exact point where research on influence turns into the old story of a people who secretly rule. A US Defense Department office made that move in 1995, in a memo on Israeli collection that cited “strong ethnic ties to Israel present in the U.S.”, and the Secretary of Defense repudiated it (attributed, JTA). The subject here is a state’s documented conduct and the American laws and votes that accommodate it - never Jews, never a bloodline, never a people. A post of 18 October 2026 - Section 230 and “conspiracy theories” about Israel

The claim, as it circulated with 229,000 views: “FBI Director Kash Patel has formally agreed to work with Democratic Rep. Jared Moskowitz to reform Section 230, which critics say will target Americans on social media who post ‘conspiracy theories’ about Israel.”

What happened - documented, and thirteen months earlier. At the House Judiciary Committee’s FBI oversight hearing on 17 September 2025, a week after Charlie Kirk was killed, Representative Moskowitz said: “There are so many conspiracy theories on what is going on with Charlie Kirk. Israel assassinated him; right? … If we want to do something, then we could talk about Section 230. We should talk about how we are going to make sure that we don’t let foreign governments poison our children’s minds.” The Director answered: “I will work with you on 230 any day.” The day before, asked in the Senate about ending Section 230, he had said “I’ve advocated for that for years.” Israel appears in the exchange as one example of a conspiracy theory about the killing; the Director’s answer is about Section 230 only.

Graded. “Formally agreed”: unsupported - there is no letter, memorandum or bill, and the FBI has no part in making law. “Will target Americans on social media who post ‘conspiracy theories’ about Israel”: unsupported. The Section 230 bills before Congress (S. 3546, December 2025; H.R. 10332, September 2026) repeal the section outright after two years; neither mentions any subject, and Moskowitz sponsors neither. Section 230 says a platform is not “treated as the publisher or speaker” of what its users post; it has never protected the person who posts, and repealing it would expose platforms to lawsuits over users’ speech, not make any speech unlawful - the First Amendment limit is unchanged. The nearest real text is H.Res. 1239 (April 2026), a non-binding resolution Moskowitz co-sponsors that condemns “antisemitic rhetoric and conspiracy theories disseminated by influential online personalities” and asks platforms to “enforce their policies”; it has no force of law, never mentions Section 230, and has not left committee. Where speech about Israel did meet state power in 2025–26, the instrument was immigration: a federal court found that the Secretaries of State and Homeland Security implemented two executive orders “in a viewpoint-discriminatory way to chill protected speech” of non-citizen academics (AAUP v. Rubio, D. Mass.; on appeal). That is the documented case, and it is not this one. The post is the 2025 exchange with “formally” added and the critics’ reading presented as the policy.
