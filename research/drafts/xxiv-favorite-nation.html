A favorite nation - Washington’s test, run against the US–Israel record

The claim put to this section, in the author’s words, is that the relationship between the United States and Israel is an unhealthy one - a foreign power’s influence operating inside American policy - and that it reaches as far as a coup, or foreign control of the United States. The first half is a reading of a record, and the record is large. The second half is a claim about a transfer of power, and it needs a document of its own. They are graded separately below, because running them together is how the documented half gets thrown out with the undocumented one.

The test is older than either country’s intelligence services, and it is the first President’s. Washington’s Farewell Address, 1796: “Against the insidious wiles of foreign influence (I conjure you to believe me, fellow-citizens) the jealousy of a free people ought to be constantly awake, since history and experience prove that foreign influence is one of the most baneful foes of republican government.” And, on the specific failure: “a passionate attachment of one nation for another produces a variety of evils. Sympathy for the favorite nation, facilitating the illusion of an imaginary common interest in cases where no real common interest exists, and infusing into one the enmities of the other, betrays the former into a participation in the quarrels and wars of the latter without adequate inducement or justification. It leads also to concessions to the favorite nation of privileges denied to others…” Eisenhower’s farewell of 1961 is the domestic twin: “we must guard against the acquisition of unwarranted influence, whether sought or unsought, by the military-industrial complex.” Washington names three things to measure - concessions, wars, and influence - and the record can be run against each.

Concessions of privileges denied to others - documented, in statute. Every certification of a US arms sale “to any country in the Middle East other than Israel shall include a determination that the sale… will not adversely affect Israel’s qualitative military edge over military threats to Israel” (22 U.S.C. §2776(h), 2008). That is a binding condition on every other state’s purchases, not a supply guarantee - and it is precisely a privilege denied to others. The declared policy sits beside it: “It is the policy of the United States… To help the Government of Israel preserve its qualitative military edge… To veto any one-sided anti-Israel resolutions at the United Nations Security Council” (22 U.S.C. §8602, 2012). The money is fixed by memorandum: $38 billion for FY2019–2028 ($33bn in Foreign Military Financing, $5bn missile defence), with Israel’s financing paid as a lump sum early in the fiscal year into an interest-bearing account at the Federal Reserve (CRS). The human-rights vetting law applied to it differently in practice: in 2024 the State Department found five Israeli units had committed gross violations of human rights and deemed four “remediated”, the fifth following in August, with no unit cut off (attributed, ProPublica and CNN; State’s position is that the same standards apply to every country).

The same concession pays the other side of the peace. Egypt has received $1.3 billion a year in military financing since 1987 under the framework that followed Camp David - “the same amount since 1987” (CRS) - and the edge requirement above governs every major sale to it, because §2776(h) reaches “any country in the Middle East other than Israel.” The state that money arms built the Octagon and the New Capital (Section X). It also holds a large population of political detainees: estimates run from about 65,000 (Arabic Network for Human Rights Information, March 2021) to 10,000–20,000 (2024) - attributed; Egypt publishes no total, and its president told CBS in January 2019, “We don’t have political prisoners.” Congress conditions $320m of the aid on human rights each year; parts were withheld or reprogrammed from FY2020–2022 funds, and in September 2024 the full amount was released, by waiver on $225m and certification on $95m (CRS). For decades the flagship of that system, Tora prison and its maximum-security “Scorpion” wing, stood about two kilometres from the Tura quarries in Section X, in the same district; it was emptied in 2021–22 for new complexes at Badr and Wadi el-Natrun, and an official decision of September 2025 proposes the old site for housing. Egypt does not hold the most political prisoners in the world - North Korea (80,000–120,000, UN Commission of Inquiry, 2014) and Xinjiang at its peak hold more - and this report does not say it does.

And the newest concession is pending, not enacted. Section 219 of H.R. 8800, the House’s FY2027 defence authorisation, would require the Secretary of Defense to “designate an executive agent… responsible for synchronizing cooperative efforts between the United States and Israel” - identifying “jointly developed or Israeli-origin technologies… for potential integration into United States systems and programs of record” and establishing “joint ventures, licensing agreements, and United States-based co-production or manufacturing partnerships with Israeli industry.” It carries no dollar figure and no export-control exemption (it requires consistency with existing law). The House passed the bill 216–212 on 22 July 2026, under a rule that did not allow a vote on an amendment to strike §219; the same bill removes the annual caps on US–Israel counter-drone and anti-tunnel cooperation, which the Senate version instead raises to $100m each. The Senate’s counterpart, §1217 of S. 4784, adds consultation with Israel’s Minister of Defense and asks for authorities to promote “the long-term integration of joint capabilities.” Neither is law as this is written. The author’s reading, carried as his: if enacted, this binds US defence research and industry to a foreign state’s interest - a betrayal of the national interest in Washington’s sense of the word. The legal limit on that reading is stated with it: treason under Article III is confined to levying war against the United States or adhering to its enemies, and a statute benefiting an ally cannot meet that definition. The charge is political and constitutional-tradition, not criminal, and it is argued with votes.

Participation in the quarrels and wars of the favorite nation - documented, in the President’s own letter. “On February 28, 2026, the United States and Israel launched military operations against targets in Iran” (Congressional Research Service). The President’s War Powers report of 2 March cited no authorisation from Congress and no finding of imminence; it rested on Article II and listed, among the purposes, “collective self-defense of our regional allies, including Israel.” Israeli and US forces “coordinated closely during active hostilities” (CRS). That the Israeli government pressed for the war over months, including a Situation Room presentation on 11 February, is attributed (New York Times); that Israel at points delayed or was told not to strike is also reported and is carried with it. The cost is documented: 18 Americans killed and 824 wounded as of 15 September, tens of billions spent, and a UN finding against the United States already on this page (Section XV). Congress voted on war powers seventeen times; three House and one Senate measure passed, as concurrent resolutions whose binding force has been doubted since INS v. Chadha (1983).

The insidious wiles of foreign influence - documented, repeatedly, and some of it adjudicated. Jonathan Pollard, arrested 21 November 1985 while passing classified material to an Israeli intelligence unit, pleaded guilty and was sentenced to life; Israel called it a rogue operation until 1998, when it acknowledged him as its agent. A Pentagon analyst, Lawrence Franklin, pleaded guilty in 2005 to passing classified information, including to two lobby employees whose own Espionage Act case was dropped in 2009. In 1962 the Justice Department told the American Zionist Council in writing that funding from the Jewish Agency made it “an agent of a foreign principal” and requested registration; by 1965 the Department accepted a filing it said “does not comprise a registration statement,” and the Council never registered. In 2024 Meta removed a network of 510 Facebook accounts, with Pages, a Group and Instagram accounts, aimed at audiences in the United States and Canada including US lawmakers, and linked it to a Tel Aviv political-marketing firm; that Israel’s Diaspora Ministry commissioned it for about $2m is attributed (New York Times), and the ministry denied it. The FBI concluded in 2019 that Israel likely placed phone-surveillance devices near the White House (attributed, Politico; Israel called it a “blatant lie”). The Commerce Department put NSO Group on its Entity List in November 2021 for supplying spyware used against government officials and journalists, and it remains there; the FBI itself bought a Pegasus licence in 2019 “for product testing and evaluation only”. And the signals relationship runs one way in a way the others do not: an NSA memorandum published in 2013 shows raw, unminimised intercepts - Americans’ communications included - shared with Israeli intelligence. On the money: AIPAC is registered as a domestic lobby, not a foreign agent; its super PAC raised $87.2m in the 2024 cycle and $109.7m in the 2026 cycle to 31 August (FEC). How members vote is on the roll: in April 2026, 40 senators backed at least one of two resolutions to block arms sales to Israel, and 59 and 63 voted them down. A vote is a recorded act. This report prints the votes and claims nothing about any member’s loyalty.

Measured against Washington’s test, the record is not soft. A statute conditions every regional arms sale on one country’s advantage; the same framework pays the other side of the peace, to a state that jails its opposition; a policy of the United States commits its Security Council veto; a war was begun jointly, with that country’s defence named in the legal basis; one of its intelligence services ran an American spy to a life sentence; the council from which its principal American lobby grew was told in writing it was a foreign agent, and never registered; and a pending bill would build its industry into American programs of record. That is the documented half, and on the page it stands at documented tier. The author’s reading - that this is the passionate attachment Washington warned against, and an unhealthy relationship - is carried at the author’s hypothesis tier, and the record gives it a great deal to stand on.

The other half is graded where it stands. “Coup” and “foreign control of the United States”: unsupported at any tier. Nothing read for this entry shows Israeli agents attempting to seize, direct or control any function of the US government. What the record holds is espionage, covert influence operations, lobbying and money, and asymmetries written into American law - channels of influence, every one of them documented, and none of them a transfer of control. The guaranteed-null rule applies in both directions: the absence of a coup record is not evidence of hidden control, and it is not evidence of innocence either; it is evidence that the claim has no document. Rung 5 - tasked and run - is claimed for no one, here as everywhere. And the Section VII guardrail is invoked by name, because this is the exact point where research on influence turns into the old story of a people who secretly rule: the subject here is a state’s documented conduct and the American laws and votes that accommodate it - never Jews, never a bloodline, never a people. Keeping that line is what keeps the documented half standing.
