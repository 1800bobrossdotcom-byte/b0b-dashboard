The request has three parts and they get three different answers.

   one of the author's own sentences on the page ("each Christ paradox and each parable is
   math") is the bridge. §4 takes each rule in turn and stops where the mapping stops.

Three of the report's rules govern this document exactly as they govern the report:

- **Guaranteed-null.** A text of tens of thousands of verses can be matched to any method.
  A parallel that any document would satisfy is evidence of nothing, so every parallel here
  is either one the page itself draws, or one that is *specific* to a named rule and could
  fail against a different document. Where I could not make a fit specific, it is not here.
- **No numerology.** Chapter numbers, verse numbers, section counts and the numbers inside
  the verses are recorded and never read for meaning. This is the most numerologised text

- **Living-person floor.** No verse is applied to a living person as judgment. The one
  verse the page applies to a person (Daniel 5:27) is applied to a dead one, as a figure,
  and the page says so.

And one posture carried over from the page: the author's theological propositions are his,
recorded as his, and this document neither adopts nor disputes them. That is what the
report does at its own close, and a comparison that did otherwise would be worth less.

## 4. Game theory — where it holds, rule by rule

The bridge is the author's, on the page, in Section XXIV: "Each Christ paradox and each
parable is math." Recorded as his. What follows tests it against the report's named rules,
which is the only place the claim can be tested without becoming the thing §3 refuses.

### 4.1 The guaranteed-null rule is a pooling equilibrium

In signalling theory a signal carries information only if types send it at different
rates. When every type sends the same signal — every PEAD is classified, every service file
is withheld — the receiver learns nothing from receiving it. That is a *pooling*
equilibrium, and the report's rule is its plain-language statement: "a silence a process
is certain to produce carries no weight in either direction." The KJV's version is Daniel
12:9 (§3.2). The rule is strong precisely because it is symmetric — it forbids reading the
seal as guilt and forbids reading it as acquittal, which is where Section II's death record
applies it.

### 4.2 The symmetry rule is likelihood consistency

A Bayesian reader updates on evidence by the same likelihood function whichever way the
evidence points. A reader who discounts adverse evidence and accepts favourable evidence of
the same quality is not updating; the ratchet runs one way and the conclusion was chosen
before the evidence arrived. The report calls this "the ratchet the symmetry rule exists to
catch." Exodus 23:2–3 is the rule (§2.2). The operating memory records the assistant
failing it in both directions — "deference to the conversational gradient rather than to
the record" — which is the honest way to say that the rule is a discipline and not a
temperament.

### 4.3 Published nulls are costly signals — and this is §8 of the operating memory

Spence's result: a signal separates honest from dishonest senders only when it costs the
dishonest sender more. Killing a specimen on the page costs an honest document little (it
never needed the specimen) and costs a propaganda document its argument. Printing a null at
the same prominence as a finding costs an honest document a paragraph and costs an
accumulating document its momentum. So the report's discipline — "every specimen killed,
every null published at equal prominence" — is a separating signal, and the sentence the
operating memory closes on is its exact game-theoretic content: **"The document's authority
does not come from what it alleges. It comes from what it refuses to allege."** Cheap talk
is the alternative: an oath, an assurance, a disclaimer. Matthew 5:37 forbids the oath and
keeps the plain statement, and James 5:12 repeats it — "let your yea be yea; and your nay,
nay." The plain statement is the costly signal because it can be checked and the oath
cannot. This is also why the author's 5 Sept instruction to remove subject-shielding
disclaimers was right on signalling grounds and not only on tone: reassurance is cheap talk
and it reads as such.

### 4.4 The standing constraints are commitment devices, and Article 94 is a refused coordination game

Schelling's insight was that binding yourself in advance can be worth more than keeping
your options open, because the binding is visible to the other side. The report's §2
constraints are that: fixed before the evidence, applied when they bite the author ("a rule
that only bites strangers is not a rule"), and published. Deuteronomy 19:15 is a
pre-committed evidentiary threshold of the same kind.

Section XXIII is the deliberate *refusal* to solve a coordination game. The report reads
the 51% threshold as a Nash equilibrium — "breaking free requires coordinated action by a

protocol," and concert of action is the aggravating element under Article 94. What it

is the text's account of what a coordination mechanism costs once installed — the tenth of
everything, itemised — and Judges 21:25 is what its absence looks like. The KJV's "Ideal
Money" is Proverbs 11:1 and 20:23, the just weight and the false balance, and the report's
instrument for the same thing is the tier. Nash's "Ideal Money" is documented on the page
as an argument for inflation-stable currency, "not a Bitcoin prophecy" — the page uses
the word *prophecy* once, and uses it to refuse one.

### 4.6 By their fruits is revealed preference

Samuelson's principle: infer what an agent values from what it does, not from what it
says. Matthew 7:16 is the same rule. The report applies it to institutions: a club
membership is a declared preference and is weighted accordingly; a cap table, a contract
award and an export licence are choices with prices attached and are documents. The
money-layer finding — that the page carries the club layer heavily and the money layer
barely, "and that is backwards" — is a revealed-preference correction to the report's own
evidence base.

### 4.7 Tetelestai — finality as the end of the incentive to misreport

This is the one the author laughed at, and the joke is precise. The "paid in full" gloss
turned the word into a receipt — a ledger entry — and the page killed it as a naming
error. What survived the correction is the grammar: perfect passive, *completed and
remains so*. And the page's closing argument is a game-theoretic one whether or not it
was written as one: "Every discipline in it … only makes sense on one assumption: that the
record already exists, and the only open question is whether it is read honestly … It is
the behaviour of a document written as though the ledger were already kept."

In game terms: a player who believes the audit is certain and already complete has no
strategy in which burying a retraction pays. Honest reporting stops being a virtue and
becomes the dominant strategy, because concealment has no remaining payoff. Ecclesiastes
8:11 states the other case — "because sentence against an evil work is not executed
speedily, therefore the heart of the sons of men is fully set in them to do evil" —
which is the discount rate on a delayed audit, and the incentive the report's posture
removes by assuming the audit is not delayed at all. The receipt reading and the
completion reading come apart exactly here: a receipt is a settlement between parties who
were bargaining, and the perfect passive is a state that was never up for negotiation.
The page's tier holds: the grammar is documented, the theology is the author's, and the
structural claim — that a document behaving as if already audited has no reason to lie
about itself — is checkable against the document's conduct. It has 33 edit notes removed
and every corrected fact kept; the specimens it killed are still on the page as killed.

## 6. Limits

1. The KJV is a 1611 translation. Where the page reads grammar it reads Greek and Hebrew;
   where this document quotes text it quotes the English, and the two are not the same
   tier.
2. Selection effect. With tens of thousands of verses a match exists for any method. The
   guard used was specificity — each fit had to be to a *named* rule and had to be one a
   different document could fail — and the fits that did not pass it were dropped, not
   softened.
3. The game-theory mappings are to standard results (Spence 1973; Schelling 1960; Nash
   1950; Lamport, Shostak and Pease 1982; Samuelson 1938) at the level of the concept. No
   model was fitted and no payoff was estimated. Where the report's Section VIII goes
   further than the concept, this document does not follow it.
4. The author's theological propositions are recorded as his throughout. This document
   neither adopts nor disputes them, which is the page's own posture at its close, and the
   comparison would be worth less under any other.
