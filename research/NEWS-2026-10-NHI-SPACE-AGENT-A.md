## A. UAP / NHI

### A1. DoW waiver for disclosures to PURSUE, 14 Sept 2026
- **Claim:** The Department of War waived NDA/SAPIA enforcement for UAP disclosures made to PURSUE.
- **Tier:** Documented. Named outlets quote the DoW release; the release itself returned 403.
- **Sources:**
  - DefenseScoop, 14 Sept: https://defensescoop.com/2026/09/14/pentagon-legal-relief-uap-whistleblowers-trump-project/
  - The Debrief, 16 Sept: https://thedebrief.org/pentagon-opens-new-pathway-for-government-insiders-to-disclose-uap-information/
- **Quotes:**
  - Release, quoted by DefenseScoop: *"This targeted legal relief establishes an authorized pipeline for systematic review, security evaluation, and potential declassification of historical and ongoing UAP data."*
  - DefenseScoop: the waiver *"supersedes civil and administrative enforcement provisions contained within Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs) and Special Access Program Indoctrination Agreements (SAPIAs) … This policy is strictly for communications with the PURSUE team."*
  - Release, quoted by DefenseScoop: *"without triggering breach-of-agreement penalties or standard NDA violations."*
  - The Debrief: it *"does not provide blanket authorization for individuals to publicly reveal protected or classified information."*
- **Limit:** DefenseScoop's lede says the waiver protects against "prosecution". The release language it quotes covers only civil and administrative enforcement provisions of agreements. The quoted text does not address criminal statutes. Whether the full release does is **unread** (403). The Disclosure Foundation's line, *"Binding law protects individuals … regardless of an NDA"*, is **attributed**: an advocate's reading of the law.
- **Official response:** "Pentagon spokespersons did not identify PURSUE representatives or respond to questions" (DefenseScoop).
- **What would move it:** the release or waiver text read at source; ODNI's implementing guidance made public (Burlison was still asking for it on 29 July: https://burlison.house.gov/media/press-releases/rep-eric-burlison-releases-statement-president-trumps-uap-nda-directive); any record of disclosures received.
- **Page:** "SAPIA" appears 0 times; the waiver is absent.

### A2. Sixth PURSUE release, 18 Sept: two points the page lacks
The page already carries this release, in XVI's "Update, 18 September 2026". Two things are new to it.

**(a) The Pentagon's own caveat on the AAWSAP DIRDs.**
- **Tier:** Documented.
- **Source:** AP via News9: https://www.news9.com/oklahoma-city-news/pentagon-releases-more-ufo-files-including-documents-on-secretive-military-program
- **Quotes:** the DIRDs are *"best understood as reference and synthesis products"* and *"should not be read as implying current validation of the concepts discussed."*
- **Context:** this includes the 2010 study of *"evidence of unintended injury to human observers by anomalous advanced aerospace systems."* All 37 DIRDs were already public by FOIA (AP). This matches `AAWSAP-FILES.md`.
- **File count:** 71 by AP (*"55 PDF files, 15 video files and one audio file"*); 72 by IBTimes (https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/new-pentagon-uap-files-2010-human-injury-report-1820757). The count is not settled.

**(b) Tremonton: the page's "two analyses, opposite conclusions" leaves out a third.**
- **What the page says:** XVI calls Blue Book (Dec 1952, pillow balloons) and NPIC ("1954", not natural phenomena) the two analyses, and says the release *"does not resolve the disagreement, it publishes it."*
- **What the released file says:**
  - Fox 5 DC, 18 Sept, quoting the file: *"later assessments 'became more confident that the objects were seabirds reflecting sunlight, citing identifiable seabirds and similar films showing seabirds as bright points of light under comparable conditions.'"* (https://www.fox5dc.com/news/pentagon-releases-sixth-batch-declassified-ufo-files)
  - The same file records dissent: a General Mills representative *"noted that the objects appeared somewhat too bright and seemed to maneuver too rapidly."*
  - IBTimes: *"later Air Force assessments settled on seagulls."*
  - Wikipedia (lead only): the Robertson Panel (1953) *"determined that it most likely showed a group of birds."*
- **Verdict:** this is the **symmetry rule failing toward the anomalous reading**. The page prints the dissent and drops the later government conclusion.
- **Also check:** the page dates the NPIC analysis to 1954. It was not verified here; if NPIC reported to the Robertson Panel, that date is wrong. Flagged for the author, not edited.

## B. New space-force units: announced, established or funded

### B1. US Space Forces–Special Operations (SPACEFOR-SOC)
- **Status: ESTABLISHED (activated); ceremony pending; funding not established.**
- **Sources:**
  - Air & Space Forces, 13 Oct (updated 14 Oct): https://www.airandspaceforces.com/space-force-activates-special-forces-command-component/
  - Stars and Stripes, 14 Oct: https://www.stripes.com/branches/space_force/2026-10-14/space-force-command-special-operations-23155128.html
- **Quotes:**
  - A&SF: *"The service activated the component Oct. 7, according to a Sept. 29 memo."*
  - Service statement, 14 Oct: *"While space capabilities are already integrated into [SOCOM] missions, this activation institutionalizes how the Space Force organizes, trains, equips, and presents forces for the special operations enterprise."*
  - Stripes: a ceremony is set for 2 Nov at MacDill, and *"Col. Peter Norsky will lead the headquarters."*
- **Funding:** in the FY2026 NDAA Congress *"blocking funding for the move until the Space Force provided more details on its plans."* Whether that condition was met is **not established.**
- **Killed:** "Space Force special forces." This is a component headquarters that presents space forces to SOCOM. It is not a new special-operations unit (naming error).
