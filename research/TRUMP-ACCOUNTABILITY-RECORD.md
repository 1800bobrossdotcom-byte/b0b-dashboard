# 1. THE FRAME, KILLED FIRST - treason is not available, and using it would cost the report

The author asked for treasonous acts. **The honest answer is that treason is the wrong instrument,
and the reason is not political caution. It is the narrowest crime in American law, and it is
narrow by deliberate constitutional design.** Every element below was verified against the primary
source rather than recalled.

## 1.1 The text, which is the whole of the definition

**Article III, Section 3, Clause 1:**

> "Treason against the United States, shall consist **only** in levying War against them, or in
> adhering to their Enemies, giving them Aid and Comfort. No Person shall be convicted of Treason
> unless on the Testimony of two Witnesses to the same overt Act, or on Confession in open Court."

Codified at **18 U.S.C. §2381**. The word *only* is doing constitutional work. **Treason is the
sole crime the Constitution itself defines**, and it did so precisely to stop governments from
calling their opponents traitors - the framers had the Statute of Treasons of 1352 and three
centuries of English abuse in front of them.

## 1.2 "Enemies" has a settled meaning, and it excludes almost everyone

**United States v. Greathouse**, 26 F. Cas. 18 (C.C.N.D. Cal. 1863) - **Field, Circuit Justice**,
a sitting Justice of the Supreme Court riding circuit. Verified verbatim from the Federal Cases
reporter:

> "The term 'enemies,' as used in the second clause, according to its settled meaning, at the time
> the constitution was adopted, **applies only to the subjects of a foreign power in a state of
> open hostility with us.** It does not embrace rebels in insurrection against their own
> government. **An enemy is always the subject of a foreign power who owes no allegiance to our
> government or country.**"

And from the same case's syllabus:

> "Treason having been defined by the constitution, **congress can neither extend nor restrict the
> crime**; its power over the subject is limited to prescribing the punishment."

**Consequence.** The United States has not formally declared war since June 1942. A foreign state
that is an adversary, a competitor, a rival, or even a party to undeclared hostilities is **not
automatically an "enemy"** in the Article III sense. Aid to such a state is not treason. It may be
espionage, it may be a violation of the Espionage Act, it may be a great many things. It is not
this.

## 1.3 "Levying war" is narrower than it sounds

**Ex parte Bollman**, 8 U.S. (4 Cranch) 75 (1807) - Marshall, C.J. confined "levying war" to the
actual waging of war. **Conspiring to levy war is not levying war.** An assemblage of men for a
treasonable purpose is required; planning, funding, and advocating are not enough. This is why the
Burr prosecution collapsed.

## 1.4 The evidentiary bar is the highest in American criminal law

**Cramer v. United States**, 325 U.S. 1 (1945) - Jackson, J.:

> "Every act, movement, deed, and word of the defendant charged to constitute treason must be
> supported by the testimony of two witnesses."

Two witnesses **to the same overt act**, not to intent, not to circumstance, not to the general
picture. And Cramer's other half, which is the part that matters here:

> "A citizen intellectually or emotionally may favor the enemy and harbor sympathies or convictions
> disloyal to this country's policy or interest, but, so long as he commits no act of aid and
> comfort to the enemy, there is no treason."

**Sympathy is not treason. Alignment is not treason. Deference is not treason. Bad judgment,
corruption, and even betrayal of an oath are not treason.**

## 1.5 The historical base rate

Roughly thirty federal treason prosecutions in the entire history of the republic. **The last
conviction was Tomoya Kawakita in 1952** (*Kawakita v. United States*, 343 U.S. 717). The last
indictment was Adam Gadahn in 2006, for al-Qaeda propaganda; he was never tried. **No American
president has ever been charged with treason, and no conduct in the public record concerning this
one has been charged as treason by any prosecutor.**

## 1.6 Why this matters to the report specifically, in the report's own vocabulary

Calling documented misconduct "treason" is **the naming error** - mistaking the sign for the thing
named. It is also **an unfalsifiable machine**: a charge that cannot be tested, because no
prosecutor will ever bring it and no court will ever rule on it, so it can never come out against
the person making it. And it hands every subject of the report a free rebuttal. **One misused legal
term discredits the twenty-four sections around it**, which is the entire reason this document
tiers everything else.

**The finding, stated plainly: treason is unsupported, and it is unsupported as a matter of law
rather than as a matter of evidence.** No further evidence could change it, because the legal
element - an enemy, meaning subjects of a foreign power in a state of open hostility - is absent.

**What replaces it.** The correct question is not "was this treason." It is: *which constitutional
provisions and criminal statutes does the documented conduct actually engage, and where does each
one stand today.* That question has real answers, several of them adverse, and they are in the
sections that follow. **They are stronger than the treason frame precisely because they are
checkable.**

# 2. THE CLOSEST THING TO THE OFFENCE FAMILY MEANT - and the charge that was never brought

**This is the line that matters most for the author's question, because the Espionage Act is what
people usually mean when they say treason. The decisive fact is what prosecutors did not charge.**

## 2.1 What was actually charged

**United States v. Trump, Nauta and De Oliveira**, No. 23-CR-80101 (S.D. Fla.), superseding
indictment 27 July 2023, **40 counts**. Counts 1 to 32 are **18 U.S.C. §793(e)** - *willful
retention* of national defense information - plus obstruction counts under §1512. Documented.

**§793(e) requires no foreign recipient and no intent to injure the United States.** It is a
retention offence. That is the whole of what was charged.

## 2.2 The charge that was not brought, which is the finding

**No count under 18 U.S.C. §794** - gathering or delivering defense information *to aid a foreign
government*. §794 carries life imprisonment or death and is the closest thing in the United States
Code to what a lay reader means by treason. **It was never charged, never sought, and never
referenced in any filing located.** Nor was §798, §793(d) (transmission), §1924, or §2071.

**Why that is probative rather than merely absent.** The prosecutors held the Bedminster audio
recording, the map episodes, the Pratt account, and an internal motive assessment. They charged
retention and nothing else. **A prosecution team with all of that evidence declining to allege
transmission to any foreign power is the strongest available evidence about the limits of the
case** - and it comes from the side that wanted to bring the case.

**Symmetry check, applied honestly.** A charging decision is not an exoneration. Prosecutors
charge what they can prove to a jury beyond reasonable doubt, not everything they believe.
**Neither reading gets to claim this fact exclusively.** But the null is real and it is adverse to
the treason frame.

## 2.3 The case ended three times without adjudicating a single fact

- **15 July 2024** - Judge Cannon dismissed the superseding indictment on an **Appointments
  Clause** ground: the Special Counsel's appointment was invalid. **Not a ruling on the conduct.**
  It also deviated from every prior court to consider the question, which is the counter-record.
- **25 November 2024** - after the election, Smith moved to dismiss as to Trump under the **2000
  OLC opinion** barring prosecution of a sitting President. **Expressly without prejudice**, and
  the bar is coextensive with his tenure. Executive-branch policy, not a court, and not a fact.
- **11 February 2025** - the co-defendants' case dismissed on prosecutorial discretion.

**Zero counts reached a jury. Zero counts were adjudicated on the merits. No conviction, no
acquittal, and - deliberately - no pardon.** Under the **guaranteed-null rule** this record is
evidence of nothing about the underlying conduct in either direction, and anyone claiming
vindication from it, in either direction, is claiming something the disposition cannot supply.

## 2.4 Volume II is still sealed

Volume I of the Special Counsel's report was released 14 January 2025. **Volume II, on the
documents case, has been withheld since 21 January 2025 and is still not public as of 7 September
2026**, with litigation continuing and the Eleventh Circuit pressing for a ruling.
*Correction to my own source: an agent characterised the seal as "made permanent" in February
2026. Independent checking shows the sealing remains under active appeal. Do not adopt
"permanent" without the docket.*

## 2.5 Where a President cannot commit the offence at all

- **10 May 2017** - the Oval Office disclosure to Lavrov and Kislyak of intelligence on an ISIS
  laptop-bomb plot, reported by the Washington Post on 15 May 2017. **Documented that it happened;
  contested whether sources and methods were compromised; no damage assessment was ever published.**
- **30 August 2019** - the posted overhead image of the Semnan launch site, retroactively
  declassified by NGA in 2022.

**The controlling counter-argument is legal and it is strong.** The President is the ultimate
classification authority (*Department of the Navy v. Egan*, 484 U.S. 518, 527). **A sitting
President cannot commit an unauthorised disclosure by disclosing.** These episodes are political
and counterintelligence facts. They are not crimes and cannot be made into crimes.

## 2.6 New in 2026, and structural

**1 April 2026** - the DOJ Office of Legal Counsel issued an opinion concluding the **Presidential
Records Act is facially unconstitutional** as an aggrandisement of the legislature at the expense
of executive autonomy. **In May 2026 Judge John D. Bates (D.D.C.) preliminarily enjoined it**,
finding challengers likely to succeed, and ordered the White House Office, the National Security
Council, the Secret Service and presidential advisers to comply with the PRA in full. **On appeal
to the D.C. Circuit; amicus briefs from former Archivists and historians filed August 2026.**
Independently verified.

**This is the single most structurally significant item on this line**: two years after a
prosecution built on records retention, the executive's own legal office declared the records
statute unconstitutional. That is a control-architecture finding, and it is fully documented.

# 3. FOREIGN MONEY - the clause that has never been construed

## 3.1 The null that governs everything else

**No court has ever ruled on the merits of whether this subject's conduct violates either
Emoluments Clause.** Three first-term suits: *CREW v. Trump*, *District of Columbia and Maryland
v. Trump*, *Blumenthal v. Trump*. Two circuit judgments were **Munsingwear-vacated in January
2021** as moot; *Blumenthal* died on standing. **Both sides claim a disposition that decided
nothing.** Guaranteed-null rule, and it cuts both ways.

**And no live litigation replaced them.** The first second-term suit squarely raising the Domestic
Emoluments Clause, *Sistrunk Seeds v. Trump*, was filed 13 May 2026 (S.D. Fla.) and has no merits
ruling. Standing is why the others are absent, not merit.

## 3.2 The primary document is better than any reporting about it

**Trump's own signed OGE Form 278e for calendar year 2025, released 30 June 2026.** Foreign
real-estate licensing income of roughly **$59.5 million**, up 71% from 2024 and close to tenfold
from 2023. Roughly **$22M UAE-linked, $9M Saudi, $5M Qatar**, and smaller Oman figures, running
largely through two Gulf developers, **Dar Al Arkan / Dar Global** and **Damac**. Total reported
income for 2025 approximately **$2.2 billion**.

**Carry the methodology caveat or do not use the numbers.** Aggregates differ by grouping - the
disclosure's own four-country figure, roughly $38M, is not the same as the $59.5M foreign
real-estate aggregate. **Cite line items and the filing, never a single headline number.**

**The legal gap is the whole question.** The payers are private developers joint-ventured with
sovereign entities. **The clause names States.** Collapsing "paid by a company partnered with a
sovereign fund" into "paid by a foreign state" is **the naming error**, and the report must not do
it.

# 5. JANUARY 6 - and the second frame that also fails, from the prosecution's own hand

## 5.1 What was charged, and it was not insurrection

**United States v. Trump**, No. 1:23-cr-00257 (D.D.C.), indictment 1 August 2023, superseded

| Count | Statute |
|---|---|
| 1 | 18 U.S.C. §371 - conspiracy to defraud the United States |
| 2 | 18 U.S.C. §1512(k) - conspiracy to obstruct an official proceeding |
| 3 | 18 U.S.C. §1512(c)(2) and §2 - obstruction of an official proceeding |
| 4 | 18 U.S.C. §241 - conspiracy against rights |

## 5.2 The Special Counsel considered insurrection and declined it - this is the finding

**Smith Report, Volume One, §II.E.** The Office considered **18 U.S.C. §2383** - rebellion or
insurrection, which carries disqualification from office as part of its penalty - and **declined
to charge it.** Its stated reasons: no direct evidence of subjective intent as to the violence;
the *Brandenburg* incitement standard is "a rigorous one"; and **no case exists in which a
criminal defendant was charged with insurrection for acting within the government to maintain
power.**

**This belongs on the page at equal prominence with anything accusatory, and it is dispositive of
a second frame.** The prosecutor with the full investigative record, who wanted to bring the case
and did bring four other counts, looked directly at insurrection and would not charge it.
**"Insurrectionist" is therefore not available as a criminal-law claim about this subject**, and
the report should say so in the prosecution's own words rather than anyone else's.

## 5.3 Trump v. Anderson - the fulcrum, and both popular readings are wrong

- **Anderson v. Griswold**, 2023 CO 63 (19 December 2023). After a **five-day trial**, the Denver
  District Court **"found by clear and convincing evidence that President Trump engaged in
  insurrection as those terms are used in Section Three."** The Colorado Supreme Court affirmed
  that finding 4-3. **The three dissents went to process, not to the conduct.**
- **Trump v. Anderson**, 601 U.S. 100 (4 March 2024), per curiam. The holding, verbatim:
  **"responsibility for enforcing Section 3 against federal officeholders and candidates rests
  with Congress and not the States."**

**The Court never reached the insurrection question.** The Colorado factual finding was **neither
affirmed nor disturbed** - it was rendered legally inoperative by a ruling about *who may enforce*.

**Both misreadings are live and both are wrong.** "The Supreme Court cleared him of insurrection"
is false. "A court found him an insurrectionist and it stands" is also false, because the judgment
carrying that finding was reversed. **The precise statement is the only honest one: a state trial
court made that finding on a clear-and-convincing standard, and the Supreme Court set the judgment
aside without reviewing it.**

## 5.4 Immunity, and the count that Fischer narrowed

- ***Trump v. United States***, 603 U.S. 593 (1 July 2024): absolute immunity for conduct within
  the President's conclusive and preclusive constitutional authority, at least presumptive immunity
  for other official acts, none for unofficial acts. **The remand it ordered was never completed.**
  Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson, JJ., dissented.
- ***Fischer v. United States***, 603 U.S. 480 (28 June 2024) narrowed §1512(c)(2) to impairment of
  records, documents or objects. **A substantial defence win.**
