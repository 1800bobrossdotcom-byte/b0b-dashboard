### A2. "Britannica 1771 called Tartaria a dominant force; later editions wiped it clean"
- **Tier: killed.** Source: the National Library of Scotland digitisation as transcribed by jimclifford.ca. This is near-primary; the scans themselves were not read.
- **1771 entry, Vol. 3, 77 words in full:** *"a vast country in the northern parts of Asia, bounded by Siberia on the north and west: this is called Great Tartary. The Tartars who lie south of Moscovy and Siberia, are those of Astracan, Circassia, and Dagistan…"*. It describes a region and its peoples, and names no state, ruler or capital. https://jimclifford.ca/early_encyclopedia_britannica/articles/1771/eb_1st_1771_012376.html
- **The same edition, entry CHINA:** *"including Chinese Tartary, a large empire … bounded by Russian Tartary on the north"*. In 1771 the name labelled land held by Russia and China. https://jimclifford.ca/early_encyclopedia_britannica/articles/1771/eb_1st_1771_004174.html
- **Later editions did not wipe it:**

  | Edition | Length | Opening |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1797 | 5,643 words | *"a very large country of Asia"* |
  | 1810 | 2,225 words | *"a name given by geographers and historians to a considerable extent of territory in Asia, lying between Russia and China"* |
  | 1860 | 153 words | *"more properly Tatary, a name applied with some degree of indefiniteness to a vast region of Central Asia"* |

  (…/1797/eb_3rd_1797_019014.html, …/1810/eb_4th_1810_016000.html, …/1860/eb_8th_1860_014209.html)
- **This is the core naming-error specimen:** a regional exonym read as proof of an empire.

### B5. CORRECTION to the page: the Younger Dryas impact is contested, not confirmed
- **The page says:** *"The geological record confirms multiple catastrophic flood events - the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis … describes a comet … impact that caused massive flooding."*
- **The cooling is documented. The impact is contested and lost ground in 2026:**
  - **Science Advances, 29 Apr 2026** (Yobo et al., Texas A&M): Debrief coverage says the osmium data *"matched expectations for volcanic aerosol deposition."* The paper itself was refused (403). https://thedebrief.org/volcanic-apocalypse-new-research-pushes-back-on-the-younger-dryas-impact-hypothesis/

  - **PLOS One retracted two Comet Research Group Younger Dryas papers (notice 11 Feb 2026).** One misidentified *"a subset of samples as comet dust when they were in fact … foraminifera."* The group's Tall el-Hammam paper was *"retracted last April"* (2025). https://retractionwatch.com/2026/03/04/controversial-comet-theory-struck-by-two-new-retractions
- **Symmetry:** proponents still publish (ScienceOpen, Feb 2026; not read). Grade it **contested**. The word "confirms" should not be attached to the impact.
