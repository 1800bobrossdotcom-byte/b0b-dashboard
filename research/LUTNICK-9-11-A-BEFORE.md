## 1. Control of Cantor Fitzgerald: Lutnick CEO from 1991, the 1992 partnership, the 1996 Delaware action and settlement

B. Gerald Cantor founded the firm. Lutnick joined in 1983 and, by the firm's own SEC filings, was President and CEO of
Cantor from 1991. In September 1992 the firm became Cantor Fitzgerald, L.P. (CFLP), and the Cantors' company CFI
became its managing general partner. The 1993 amendments, approved 3 June 1993, barred CFI and its affiliates from
competing with the partnership. The first Delaware action was filed on 6 March 1996 and settled in May 1996. In the
settlement CFI exchanged its general partnership units for limited partnership units, which left Lutnick's company,
CF Group Management, as managing general partner. Cantor died in July 1996. Iris Cantor and CFI later sued Lutnick and
three other Cantor officers in federal court, alleging they had concealed the non-compete during that settlement. The
suit was dismissed as time-barred, and the dismissal was affirmed on 16 December 2002. In a separate 1998 Chancery
action, the Chancery Court found CFI's own conduct to be an "egregious breach".

## 8. Circulating "before" claims: moving staff, warnings, backups made just before

Searches for a circulating claim that Cantor moved staff, was warned, or built backups "just before" found **no such
claim at any named source**. Each formulation returned only the recovery coverage. So the claim cannot be quoted or
killed as a specimen; it is a null. What exists is a documented fact that such a claim could be built on, and it is
graded here so that it cannot be:
- **"Backups made just before."** The third data centre opened in February 2001 (node 4). That is seven months before,
  and it was **published in an SEC filing on 6 March 2001**. Off-site disaster recovery was already in the CFTC's record in
  1998. Redundancy across the sector dates to 1987 and 1993 (Fortune). The other large WTC tenant was "fully operational
  less than 48 hours after" (Fortune). The **symmetry rule** applies: if Cantor's backup is read as foreknowledge, every
  firm's is, and the sector's practice explains both. The **guaranteed-null rule** also applies: a firm with backups
