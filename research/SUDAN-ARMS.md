# Who is arming the Sudan war — tiered

**21 September 2026. Research, unpublished.** Sources fetched by the spider and held in
`research/sources/ledger.jsonl`; refusals and one decoy recorded.

The war: Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF, Abdel Fattah al-Burhan) against the Rapid Support Forces
(RSF, Mohammad Hamdan Daglo, "Hemedti"), since 15 April 2023. Both sides are under a UN arms
embargo for Darfur (resolution 1591, 2005). The page carries the war as one Section XV entry and
one September 2026 timeline line, and nothing on who supplies it.

---

## 1. The documented layer — primary documents read in full

**RSF side.**
- **OFAC, 7 January 2025** (Treasury release, ledgered): Hemedti designated under E.O. 14098. In
  the same action Treasury designated **Capital Tap Holding L.L.C., "a UAE-based holding company
  that manages 50 companies over ten countries, [which] has provided the RSF with money and
  military equipment,"** its owner-manager Abu Dharr Abdul Nabi Habiballa Ahmmed, two Capital Tap
  subsidiaries, **Creative Python L.L.C.** ("a cut-out to obfuscate Hemedti's business dealings"),
  **Al Zumoroud and Al Yaqoot Gold & Jewellers L.L.C.** ("a UAE-based gold purchasing company …
  has purchased gold from Sudan, presumably for the benefit of the RSF, and subsequently
  transported it to Dubai"; the RSF procurement director "maintained access to AZ Gold's bank
  account in the UAE, which held millions of dollars"), Al Jil Al Qadem (shareholder of the
  RSF-controlled bank Alkhaleej, designated 31 Jan 2024), and Horizon Advanced Solutions.
  Earlier designations cited in the same release: **Algoney Hamdan Daglo Musa, "the RSF's
  procurement director and brother of RSF leader Hemedti," 8 Oct 2024**; Abdelrahim Hamdan
  Dagalo, 6 Sept 2023; GSK Advanced Business Co, 28 Sept 2023. Same day, the Secretary of State
  **determined that the RSF has perpetrated genocide in Darfur** — a State Department
  determination, not a court finding.
- The procurement geography Treasury names is therefore **the United Arab Emirates**: every
  company in the RSF network it sanctioned is UAE-based, and the gold goes to Dubai. Treasury does
  not say the Emirati state supplied the weapons; it says UAE-based companies did. That
  distinction is the whole tier line below.

**SAF side.**
- **OFAC, 16 January 2025** (ledgered): Burhan designated. The SAF's procurement arm, the
  **Defense Industries System (DIS)**, was designated June 2023 and "has sought to procure weapons
  and equipment through unofficial means … through ostensibly private companies working on its
  behalf." Designated with Burhan: **Ahmad Abdalla, a Sudanese-Ukrainian national and DIS
  official, Chief Operating Officer of Portex Trade Limited (Hong Kong), who "coordinated the
  acquisition of Iranian-made UAVs from an Azerbaijani defense company for shipment to Sudan."**
  Secretary Blinken had determined in December 2023 that members of the SAF committed war crimes.
- So the one documented SAF supply chain in a primary document runs **Iran → an Azerbaijani
  company → a Hong Kong shell → Sudan**, brokered by a Sudanese-Ukrainian national.

**The court.**
- **ICJ, Sudan v. United Arab Emirates (case 197), Order of 5 May 2025** (icj-cij.org, ledgered):
  the Court **rejected Sudan's request for provisional measures and removed the case from the
  General List** for manifest lack of jurisdiction — the UAE holds a reservation to Article IX of
  the Genocide Convention. **This is not a finding on the merits either way.** Anyone citing it
  as "the ICJ cleared the UAE" or "the ICJ heard the evidence" is committing a naming error.

**The UN Panel.**
- The **Panel of Experts (1591 Committee) final report was released 15 January 2025** covering
  developments since 12 March 2023 and including "the proliferation of weapons and violations of
  the arms embargo" (Security Council Report, Jan 2025 forecast, ledgered). The Panel's **January
  2024** report is the one widely reported as finding the accusations of UAE arms flights via
  Amdjarass, Chad, "credible." **The report itself was not obtained from here:** `docs.un.org`
  returns a 4 KB JavaScript viewer shell with HTTP 200 for `S/2024/65` — a decoy 200 in the
  spider's sense, recorded as such. Until the PDF is read, the Panel's UAE finding is
  **attributed**, carried on the reporting of it.

## 3. Symmetry — what runs against the dominant reading

- **The UAE says ammunition transits its territory to the SAF too:** on 30 April 2025 Emirati
  authorities said they had intercepted millions of rounds at a UAE airport bound for the SAF;
  the SAF denied it. Attributed, and printed because it is the one adverse claim in the
  Emirati direction.
- **The ICJ dismissal is a jurisdiction ruling, not an acquittal** — and equally not a
  conviction. Neither side may spend it.
- **Treasury's own language is corporate, not sovereign.** It names UAE-based companies and a
  Sudanese owner. The step from "UAE-based companies" to "the UAE government" is the attributed
  layer's, resting on the flights reporting and the Panel — which is why the Panel PDF matters
  and why its absence from the ledger is recorded rather than glossed.

## 4. The money layer, which is the report's own thesis

The RSF is financed by **gold**: Treasury's AZ Gold designation is the documented link (gold from
Sudan, transported to Dubai, an RSF procurement director on the account). The attributed layer
adds that the UAE is the primary purchaser of gold from RSF-held areas, and that in 2025 the UAE,
Kenya and Ethiopia each published gold production figures roughly double the prior year's. A cap
table and a customs ledger beat a club membership; here the customs ledger is the Dubai gold
trade, and it is where a records request would go.
