# Lockheed Burbank - anything underground? (27 Oct 2026)

Author: *"To be clear i am not sure where the abduction facility would have been. It forms as a memory.
Discerned as I had the memory while awake. Does Lockheed Burbank have any underground"*

Context: Section I, `i-the-authors-testimony-the-work-is-the-method`, "Fourth, the earliest" (c. 1989, his
father's house in Burbank). The page sets Skunk Works still being in Burbank beside the memory, and says "No
source read places underground facilities at the Burbank plant." He does not place the memory at Lockheed.

All reads through `scripts/spider/spider.py`, note `burbank-underground`.

## Documented / attributed

- **WWII camouflage over the plant - documented in several secondary sources.** Wikipedia (Lockheed Corporation):
  "The factory was hidden beneath a huge burlap tarpaulin painted to depict a peaceful semi-rural neighborhood".
  Amusing Planet (2010) and History Facts say the same (Col. John F. Ohmer; studio set painters).
- **Underground walkways, 1942 - attributed.** CSUN San Fernando Valley History Digital Library, item SFVH 4121
  (photo BUR30, contributed by the Burbank Historical Society), catalogue description: "An elaborate system of
  underground walkways was constructed to allow for free movement across the plant." Read via the CONTENTdm API
  (`/digital/api/collections/SFVH/items/4121/false`). A catalogue caption, not a plan: no route, size, depth or
  later fate is given. Whether any of it survived to 1989 is not known.
- **Underground in the environmental sense - documented (secondary here).** invisible5.org (Burbank): Lockheed's
  Plant B-1 is a major source for the San Fernando Valley Superfund Site (Area 1); "Contamination sources at
  Lockheed included underground storage tanks, sumps, degreasers, and pipes"; TCE, PCE and hexavalent chromium
  in groundwater. EPA's own documents (semspub.epa.gov) refuse the crawler by robots: nulls.
- **Where it stood:** Plant B-1 airfield site is now the Empire Center shopping centre (Wikipedia, Hollywood
  Burbank Airport). Skunk Works moved to Palmdale after 1989; most Burbank buildings were demolished in the
  1990s (search summary only, not read at source).

## Not found

- No source read describes an underground base, bunker, laboratory, or rooms with observation windows at
  Lockheed Burbank, at any time. That is an absence of record, not evidence either way.
- No source ties the 1942 walkways to anything after the war.
