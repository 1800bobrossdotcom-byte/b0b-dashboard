## 1. The screenshot: "Patel has formally agreed to work with Moskowitz to reform Section 230, which critics say will target Americans who post 'conspiracy theories' about Israel"

**Verdict: a real exchange from 13 months earlier, recirculated with "formally" added. No document exists, and no 230 bill targets speech about Israel.**

- **Documented (printed hearing record, House Judiciary, 17 Sept 2025, Serial No. 119-35, govinfo CHRG-119hhrg61655):**
  - Moskowitz: "There are so many conspiracy theories on what is going on with Charlie Kirk. Israel assassinated him; right? … If we want to do something, then we could talk about Section 230. We should talk about how we are going to make sure that we don't let foreign governments poison our children's minds."
  - Patel: "I will work with you on 230 any day."
  - Israel appears there as one example of a Kirk conspiracy theory. Patel's answer concerns §230 only.
- **Patel held the position already.** The day before, Graham asked him in the Senate about sunsetting §230, and he answered "I've advocated for that for years" (Rev transcript; that hearing was on 16 Sept).
- **"Formally": unsupported.** There is no letter, MOU or bill, and the FBI has no legislative role.
- **The bills that exist are flat repeals and name no subject.** S.3546 (17 Dec 2025) and H.R.10332 (10 Sept 2026) both take effect two years after enactment. Moskowitz sponsors neither.
- **The critics' best documented case is H.Res. 1239** (30 Apr 2026, 22 sponsors including Moskowitz, in committee).
  - It is a nonbinding sense-of-the-House resolution condemning "antisemitic rhetoric and conspiracy theories disseminated by influential online personalities".
  - Its one ask of platforms is to "enforce their policies against hate speech".
  - It does not mention §230. It has no force of law.
- **What §230 does:** a platform is not "treated as the publisher or speaker" of a user's post, and good-faith moderation is protected. It has never shielded the author of a post. Repealing it would expose platforms, not posters, and the First Amendment limit is unchanged.
- **Where speech about Israel actually met state power, 2025–26: non-citizens' visas.**
  - *AAUP v. Rubio*, D. Mass., held that the Secretaries implemented EO 14161 and EO 14188 "in a viewpoint-discriminatory way to chill protected speech" (802 F. Supp. 3d at 175; judgment 22 Jan 2026; on appeal).
  - This is documented and real, but it concerns immigration enforcement, not §230.
- **Lineage of the claim:** @ShadowofEzra (18 Sept 2025), then SGT Report, then the Oct 2026 versions, which add "formally" with no new document. The two Oct 2026 post ids decode to 18 Oct 2026.

## 5. USS Liberty: what is documented, and where intent stands

**Documented, US contemporaneous verdicts:**
- **The US note, 10 June 1967 (FRUS XIX d256):** "quite literally incomprehensible … military recklessness reflecting wanton disregard for human life". In July the US softened its own note: Rostow deleted "wanton" (d352).
- **Clifford to the President, 18 July (d373):** "does not reflect that the Israeli high command made a premeditated attack on a ship known to be American". The mistaken-identity account "is unbelievable". The attack was "a flagrant act of gross negligence". The high-command theory would need "a degree of access … which in all likelihood can never be achieved".
- **CIA, 21 June (d317):** "not made in malice toward the US and was by mistake, but … indicative of gross negligence". CIA, 1977 (d516): "gross stupidity and negligence but not malicious intent".
- **The Navy Court of Inquiry:** "It was not the responsibility of the Court to rule on the culpability of the attackers, and no evidence was heard from the attacking nation." Vance's account (d324): the members "believe the attack resulted from a failure within the Israeli communications system and not from premeditation. But in his view, on the evidence we have, we should not so state."
- **NSA Deputy Director Tordella, on Israel's report (d424 footnote):** "A nice whitewash for a group of ignorant, stupid and inept xxx."

**Israel's account, documented:** "not in malice … innocent mistake" (its 1967 inquiry, d307).

**Deliberate intent:**
- **Testimony:** Capt. Ward Boston's 2004 affidavit (150 Cong. Rec. E1886): "Both Admiral Kidd and I believed with certainty that this attack … was a deliberate effort to sink an American ship and murder its entire crew." He says Johnson and McNamara ordered the court to conclude "mistaken identity".
- **Attributed:** the 2003 Moorer "Independent Commission" (private). It carries a lobby-control framing, recorded as a property of that source.
- **Contested, and the US record is split inside itself:** Clifford, CIA, State INR and the court members found no premeditation. Tordella called Israel's report a whitewash, and Boston says the court's finding was ordered.

**Not documented here:**
- the wounded count: NSA refused, and the Navy history site failed TLS;
- Rusk's memoir line (the book was not read).

The 9th Circuit held on 24 Aug 2026 that the withheld House Appropriations report is a congressional record outside FOIA.
