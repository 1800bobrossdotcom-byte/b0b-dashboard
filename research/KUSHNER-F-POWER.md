# kushner — F (power: a private citizen brokering state deals)

Researched 26 Oct 2026. Research only; nothing goes on the page from this pass. Kushner is living and uncharged.

**Summary.** The question was why a private citizen brokers state-level deals, and by what mechanism. The answer
on the record is short: **the President's own Article II power over foreign relations, exercised through a person
he chooses, with no written instrument that anyone has produced.**

*Documented:*
- **2017.** OLC's 20 Jan 2017 opinion let the President appoint his son-in-law to the White House Office despite
  the anti-nepotism statute. It did so on the premise that such an appointee **is bound by the criminal
  conflict-of-interest laws**. It also gave the President a second door: to consult a relative "on an unofficial,
  ad hoc basis" with no status and no restrictions. In 2017–21 he used the first door (Senior Adviser, OGE
  filings; `KUSHNER-B-GOVERNMENT.md` §1, §4d).
- **2025–26.** No appointment instrument, executive order, SGE designation, OGE 278 or ethics agreement for
  Kushner has been found.
  - EO 14311 (30 June 2025) creates one Special Envoy for Peace Missions, and Witkoff is that envoy, with OGE 278e
    filings.
  - Kushner's title exists only orally ("we're making Jared an envoy also", 19 Feb 2026). The 16 Jan 2026 White
    House statement names him on the Board of Peace's Executive Board and its Gaza Executive Board.
  - CREW asked the White House and State on 20 July 2026 for his disclosures; both said they have none (CREW's
    complaint).
- **The law that would constrain a private envoy does not reach him as he is used:**
  - the Logan Act bars only correspondence "without authority of the United States". It has produced two
    indictments since 1799 (1803 and 1853) and no conviction;
  - 22 U.S.C. §2651a(j) requires Senate consent for envoys "at the Department of State" who exercise significant
    authority, and he is not at State;
  - the SGE and ethics statutes attach only to a person who is "retained, designated, appointed, or employed".
- **OLC's own 1977 test (the "Mr. A" opinion)** says that once an informal adviser chairs meetings of agency
  employees and coordinates an administration policy, he is doing a governmental function and "should be formally
  appointed and take an oath of office". OLC also says the act of appointment may not be required where it was
  omitted "for the purpose of avoiding" the conflict laws.
- **Objection is on record, and it is all from one side:**
  - CREW: the 20 Feb 2026 statement, the 11 Mar 2026 letter, and the suit, D.D.C. 1:26-cv-03580, filed 14 Oct 2026.
  - House Democrats' letters (beat B).
  - The administration's answer: "volunteer", "private citizen", paying his own expenses, cleared by unnamed
    "government ethics attorneys". The White House called CREW "unserious, Trump-deranged".

*Not established:* any written delegation of authority to Kushner; any designation as an SGE; the content of the
ethics review his lawyer cites; any security clearance in the second term; any ruling in CREW's suit.

## 1. The 2017 OLC opinion: the door the President chose then, and the second door

- **20 Jan 2017.** OLC (Deputy AAG Koffsky) advised the Counsel to the President that 3 U.S.C. §105(a), the White
  House Office hiring authority, overrides 5 U.S.C. §3110.
- Its stated premise is that White House Office appointees remain under 18 U.S.C. §§203, 205, 207, 208 and 209.
- It names the alternative: the President may consult relatives "as private citizens" with no status. Its own
  footnote 7 then limits that alternative by citing the 1977 "Mr. A" opinion (§2).
- **CRS (6 Aug 2018)** records that the opinion departed from earlier precedent holding that the statute barred
  relatives as White House staff.

## 2. The 1977 "informal adviser" test: OLC's own line between advising and governing

- **24 Feb 1977, OLC (Harmon) to the Attorney General.**
  - Daily informal advice does not make "Mr. A" an employee.
  - But once he "called and chaired" meetings of agency employees and coordinated an administration policy, he was
    performing a governmental function, should be designated an SGE (or a regular employee if more than 130 days),
    and should be formally appointed.
  - An act of appointment may not be essential where the parties omitted it to avoid the conflict laws.
- The 2017 opinion cites this test (footnote 7). It is the executive branch's own published standard for exactly
  Kushner's 2025–26 posture.

## 6. The law that governs private diplomacy, and why none of it binds him as he is used

- **Logan Act, 18 U.S.C. §953.** It reaches only those acting "without authority of the United States", and the
  President's request supplies that authority.
  - Enforcement, per CRS (12 Jan 2018): two indictments since 1799 (Flournoy 1803; Levy 1853), no conviction,
    none since. The older CRS report (RL33265) found "one indictment".
  - Administrative enforcement has been through passports.
  - State's 1975 view: the clear intent is to bar "unauthorized persons".
  - Alleged violators named by critics: Jackson 1984, Perot, Wright, the 47 Senators in 2015. "There have
    apparently been no official sanctions taken in any of these instances."
  - **The Logan Act is a check on private diplomacy against the government, not on private diplomacy for it.**
- **The only Kushner episode in the record where the "without authority" question was live is 22 Dec 2016.** He
  "led the effort for the Transition Team" to delay or defeat UNSC res. 2334 while the sitting administration
  abstained (Mueller Vol. I).
  - The report quotes no Logan Act analysis (a text search of Vol. I for "Logan" finds none).
  - No charge followed for him. Flynn's plea was under §1001, about his statements to the FBI.
- **22 U.S.C. §2651a(j)** (as amended, Dec 2021).
  - It bars designating any envoy "at the Department" who exercises significant authority without Senate consent.
  - A 180-day temporary exception requires notice to the committees.
  - **It speaks only of the Department of State.** A White House-based envoy (EO 14311's office sits in the White
    House Office under 3 U.S.C. §105) and an untitled volunteer fall outside its words.
  - Witkoff's own timeline: State special envoy 1/2025–6/2025, then the new White House office from 30 June 2025
    (his 278e). No source read gives a reason for the move, and **the inference that it avoided §2651a(j)(3)'s
    180-day limit is not established.**
- **22 U.S.C. §3942.** Ambassadors and ambassadors at large are by advice and consent. AFSA's tracker (20 Oct
  2026) lists Russia, Ukraine, Qatar, the UAE and Saudi Arabia as VACANT (Saudi: a nominee pending since 14 Sept
  2026), and Israel as filled (Huckabee).
  - **Naming trap:** "France & Monaco: Charles Kushner" is the father.
- **SGE, 18 U.S.C. §202(a).** It applies only if he is "retained, designated, appointed, or employed". OLC 1977 says
  the omission of that act can itself be disregarded (§2).
- **FARA.** The question was raised only as Wyden–Raskin's Oct 2024 request for a special counsel (beat A §); no
  finding. FARA registration records were not searched in this pass (null).

## Mechanism, stated plainly

- **What grants the power.**
  - The President's Article II authority to conduct foreign relations (*Zivotofsky*, as CREW itself pleads) and to
    choose whom he sends.
  - The foreign side's acceptance of the person as the President's representative. Kremlin: "two representatives
    of U.S. President Donald Trump". Egypt's presidency: "envoys", per the complaint.
  - **A position inside the official channel:** the Board of Peace seats, State staff support (Rubio), and
    Witkoff's EO 14311 office as his working base.
  - The power is personal and revocable at will. Nothing written that confers it has been produced.
- **What would constrain it, and why it does not bite.**
  - *Logan Act:* inapplicable to anyone acting with the President's authority, and dormant since 1853 in any case.
  - *Appointments Clause, §2651a(j), §3942:* they attach to an "officer", a State envoy or an ambassador, and he
    holds no designation that triggers them.
  - *SGE and conflict statutes (§§202–209), the Ethics Act and the Presidential Records Act:* they attach to
    someone "retained, designated, appointed, or employed", and the administration says he is none of these.
  - The executive branch's own 1977 test says that line is crossed by coordinating government work, and that an
    omitted appointment may be disregarded. **Only a court (CREW's suit) or the executive itself can apply it.**
    Neither has.
  - *Political check:* the Senate has not been asked to confirm him. The minority letters have gone unanswered
    (Raskin: "You have not responded").
- **What is missing from the record:**
  - any delegation instrument;
  - any SGE designation or oath;
  - any OGE 278 or ethics agreement, recusal or waiver for 2025–26;
  - the "government ethics attorneys'" review;
  - any second-term clearance record;
  - records-preservation arrangements (CREW alleges non-compliance "on information and belief" only);
  - any government answer to CREW's suit.
- **So the accurate answer to "why does a civilian broker state deals" is this:** a President may send whoever he
  trusts. The safeguards Congress wrote are keyed to formal status. Declining a title and a salary is what keeps
  those safeguards from attaching. That is documented as a structure; no intent is documented. Whether it is
  lawful is the open question in 1:26-cv-03580.
