# USAF Directed-Energy / Pulsed-Power Weapons: The Unclassified Paper Trail

**Scope:** verify the claim that USAF directed-energy/pulsed-power capability development ran openly in the peer-reviewed and DTIC/OSTI literature, under program names that stated the intent plainly, and went largely unread. Anchor document (already verified, not re-verified here): OSTI 7369133, Degnan et al., "Compact toroid formation, compression, and acceleration," *Physics of Fluids B* 5(8), 2938–2958 (1993), DOI 10.1063/1.860681.

Status key: **CONFIRMED** (primary source in hand), **CORROBORATED** (two independent secondary sources agree, primary not directly fetchable), **UNVERIFIED FROM HERE** (network/paywall blocked a would-be primary source), **UNSUPPORTED** (claim checked and not substantiated).

## 1. The "1965 USAF Kugelblitz" claim — VERDICT: NOT SUBSTANTIATED AS AN AIR FORCE PROGRAM. REAL DOCUMENT EXISTS, BUT IT IS ARMY, NOT USAF, AND ITS AIR FORCE COUSIN WAS NOT A WEAPONS PROGRAM.

A real, unclassified 1965 U.S. government report on weaponizing ball lightning ("kugelblitz") exists:

- **DTIC AD0370246**, *Survey of Kugelblitz Theories for Electromagnetic Incendiaries*, W.B. Lyttle and C.E. Wilson, December 1965, 92 pp. Citation page: https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/citations/AD0370246 (returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch from this network — **UNVERIFIED FROM HERE** for full-text; bibliographic existence corroborated independently by a DTIC-derived reprint listing at gyanbooks.com, which confirms title/date/page count, and by a Scribd upload titled "Survey of Kugelblitz Theories (US Army Unclassified)" — https://www.scribd.com/document/159242153/Survey-of-Kugelblitz-Theories-Us-Army-Unclassified, itself unfetchable but its title string is a direct corroborating data point). Attempted a direct PDF pull from `apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/AD0370246.pdf`: HTTP 403, 1,484-byte HTML error page returned (SHA-256 of that error page: `918a4224bb028c3296c08ed93d1926abfd2b292544693e689a73dae254177dd0` — recorded for completeness; **this is not the document**, just DTIC's block page).
- Sponsor: **Edgewood Arsenal, "New Concepts Division / Special Projects"** — a **U.S. Army** Chemical Corps installation, not an Air Force one. Authors Lyttle and Wilson were contractor staff at **Melpar, Inc.** working under an Army contract. Multiple independent secondary accounts (Secret Projects Forum thread title "Ball Lightning: Harness Cavalier and 'electromagnetic incendiaries,'" and a Journal News Online piece on U.S. government UFO/ball-lightning research) converge on Edgewood Arsenal as sponsor; none identify an Air Force sponsor for this specific report.
- Stated purpose (per secondary accounts quoting the report): to "review the theory and experimental data on ball lightning... and if it is a high energy phenomenon define an effective theoretical and experimental program to develop a potential incendiary weapon."

Separately, there **was** genuine Air Force ball-lightning research, but it is a different program with a different purpose:

- **Air Force Cambridge Research Laboratories (AFCRL), Hanscom Field**, researcher **Edmond M. Dewan**, published a ~60-page government report on kugelblitz in **March 1964**, with a follow-up in 1965; no further work on the topic at Hanscom after 1966 (Cabinet Magazine, "Eyewitness Accounts of Kugelblitz," https://www.cabinetmagazine.org/issues/3/dewan.php). This work was **atmospheric/plasma-physics research into a natural phenomenon** — soliciting eyewitness accounts, attempting lab reproduction with colleague Martin Stiglitz, evaluating Kapitza's microwave-plasma theory versus a thermonuclear-fusion theory. Nothing in the available account frames Dewan's AFCRL work as a weapons-development program, and the source explicitly states it found **no reference** to a separate USAF weaponization study.

**Verdict:** the "1965 USAF Kugelblitz weapons study" as commonly repeated online **conflates two real but distinct threads** — an Army-sponsored contractor weaponization survey (AD0370246, Edgewood Arsenal) and unrelated Air Force basic-science ball-lightning research (Dewan/AFCRL, Hanscom). The Army document is real and its stated goal is genuinely a weapon-development recommendation; attributing it to the Air Force is not supported by the sourcing found. Treat any claim of "USAF Kugelblitz weapon program" as **folklore built on a real but misattributed document** unless a primary DTIC-fetched copy of AD0370246 itself is shown to name an Air Force sponsor (it was not possible to confirm the sponsor line directly from the primary document on this network — full-text access was blocked; the Army attribution rests on convergent secondary sourcing, not a primary-document read).

## 2. Shiva Star, Kirtland AFB — CONFIRMED (primary DTIC + AFRL sourcing)

- **Primary source:** DTIC **ADA637433**, *SHIVA STAR Inductive Pulse Compression System*, Air Force Weapons Laboratory, Kirtland AFB, NM, dated **1 June 1983** (Internet Archive host, full record fetched: https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA637433). Describes the system, at the time, as "the world's highest energy, fast capacitor bank": **9.5 MJ at 120 kV**, short-circuit current approaching **90 MA**, ~3 µs current risetime compressed via an inductive-store/opening-switch stage (exploding-fuse opening switches, ~10 nH store charged to 35 MA, interrupted in hundreds of ns) to deliver fast rise to the load. This is the authoritative contemporaneous figure — matches the "reported ~9.4 MJ" framing in the task closely (9.4–9.5 MJ range recurs across sources; AFRL's own 2014 public-affairs copy rounds it to "nearly 10 million joules," see below).
- **Build history:** Wikipedia's sourced account (citing Turchi & Baker, *J. Appl. Phys.* 44(11), 1973, on electromagnetic implosion, plus an AFRL retrospective "30 years of Pulsed Power R&D in AFRL Kirtland's Building 322") gives an evolution: **SHIVA I (1974)**, four-arm cross configuration, 1 MJ/100 kV; **SHIVA II (1979)**, 1.9 MJ/120 kV; reconfigured to the six-arm **star** layout in **1982** — which is the point at which "Shiva Star" as such is dated, matching the task's "reported 1982" build date; DTIC ADA637433 (1983) then documents the completed 9.5 MJ system. I could not independently pull the "30 years of Pulsed Power R&D" AFRL retrospective as a primary document on this network (searches for it returned no direct hit) — its content here is **UNVERIFIED FROM HERE**, relayed only via Wikipedia's citation of it.
- **Who built/operated it:** the **Air Force Weapons Laboratory** (predecessor of Phillips Laboratory, now AFRL's Directed Energy Directorate), Kirtland AFB, NM — confirmed as corporate author on DTIC ADA637433 and consistent across every source checked.
- **What it was used for:** originally **x-ray radiation-environment simulation for nuclear-survivability testing** of defense electronics (per DTIC ADA637433 and secondary AFRL history); in the SDI era (late 1980s–1990s) it powered the **MARAUDER** compact-toroid experiments (see §3); from the 2000s onward it has been the driver for **magnetized target fusion (MTF)** work — FRX-L hardware reconstructed in the Shiva Star high-bay (~2006), the **FRC Compression/Heating Experiment (FRCHX)**, an AFRL/LANL collaboration (~2007–2016) that reported 540 T magnetic compression, and, per an October 2025 report, continued liner-implosion/Z-pinch experiments at 12 MA/20 µs risetime exploring sub-ignition magneto-inertial fusion regimes.
- **Current status:** **operational**, still under AFRL's Directed Energy Directorate at Kirtland. An official Kirtland AFB public-affairs article ("Tour of Shiva Star sends powerful message," 25 Apr 2014, https://www.kirtland.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/817735/tour-of-shiva-star-sends-powerful-message/) — a **.mil primary/official source** — describes it as active, storing "nearly 10 million joules," producing "120,000 volts and 10 million amps in 10 millionths of a second," used for demonstration/education as well as research. Search results referencing 2025 liner-compression experiments corroborate continued active use, though I did not locate a document dated later than 2025 confirming present (late-2026) operational status; treat "currently operational" as **CORROBORATED, last directly confirmed ~2025**, not confirmed as of this writing.

## 3. MARAUDER — CONFIRMED core facts (anchor document + corroborating OSTI record); funding line and precise end-state are UNVERIFIED FROM HERE

- **Name:** MARAUDER = **"Magnetically Accelerated Ring to Achieve Ultra-high Directed Energy and Radiation"** — verbatim from the anchor abstract (OSTI 7369133 / DOI 10.1063/1.860681), already verified per task instructions.
- **Organization:** **High Energy Plasma Division, Phillips Laboratory, Kirtland AFB**, with **Physical Sciences Inc.** (Alexandria, VA) and **Maxwell Laboratories** (Albuquerque) as contractor partners (anchor document affiliation line, confirmed again on the OSTI 7369133 record page itself).
- **Span:** commonly reported as **1988–1993** (multiple secondary sources, e.g. Wikipedia's MARAUDER article) as the core experimental campaign; but the OSTI record trail extends the underlying compact-toroid work past that window under the same Kirtland/Phillips Lab group: **OSTI 121551**, Degnan, Baca, Bell et al., "Compression of compact toroids in conical-coaxial geometry," *Fusion Technology* 27(2), **March 1995**, same institution (Phillips Lab, Kirtland AFB) — the OSTI record does not use the MARAUDER name explicitly, but is the same author group, same apparatus family, and a direct technical continuation of the 1993 anchor paper's compression/acceleration work. This supports "absorbed into ongoing pulsed-power/toroid research" rather than a hard 1993 stop.
- **Predecessor tie:** the program built on the **Ring Accelerator Experiment (RACE)** at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (~1991) — a coaxial magnetized plasma gun / precompression cone / acceleration section / focusing cone, using 200 kJ (fast) and 300 kJ (slow) formation banks plus a 250 kJ/120 kV acceleration bank; multiple secondary sources state "the first MARAUDER experiment was motivated by RACE."
- **Reported performance (secondary sourcing, not independently primary-verified here):** by 1993, acceleration of 1–2 mg plasma toroids to accelerations reported as up to ~10^11 g within a 1-m-diameter apparatus, each shot's energy compared to roughly 5 lb of TNT equivalent. These figures come from a Wikipedia synthesis citing Sovinec (1990), McLean (1991), and the Degnan et al. 1993 anchor paper; I did not independently re-derive them from the primary DOI text (already verified per task, so not re-opened here).
- **Funding line:** **UNVERIFIED FROM HERE.** MARAUDER-era compact-toroid work at Phillips Lab sits within the Strategic Defense Initiative Organization's (SDIO) **Innovative Science and Technology (IST)** directorate structure — a DTIC document on the IST directorate's proposal-selection/funding process turned up in search, but I was not able to pull a dollar figure or program-element number tying IST funding specifically to MARAUDER by name. No specific budget figure should be treated as established.
- **Termination/classification/successor:** **not cleanly documented in the open sources reached.** No source found states MARAUDER was formally terminated versus quietly folded into successor toroid/FRC work, nor does any source state it was reclassified. What the record does support: the same hardware (Shiva Star) and largely the same research group at the same institution moved from MARAUDER-branded compact-toroid acceleration work (through 1993, per the anchor paper) into unbranded/differently-branded compact-toroid compression work (1995, OSTI 121551) and then into the explicitly fusion-framed **FRCHX/MTF** program from the mid-2000s onward (§2). Whether that later fusion-energy framing is a genuine re-purposing toward civilian fusion, a classification-driven rebranding, or simply the natural evolution of a basic-plasma-physics research line cannot be determined from unclassified sourcing alone — **this is a gap in the record, not a finding**.
- Non-primary color (flagged, not relied on for facts): a Substack post ("The government learned to launch self-organizing plasma — then MARAUDER went dark") and a secretmilitarytechnology.com "dossier" both frame MARAUDER as having "gone dark" — these are enthusiast/OSINT-blog sources, not DTIC/OSTI/peer-reviewed, and are noted here only as an indication of what the folklore claims, not as evidence the claim is true.

## 4. Other unclassified programs in the same family — self-describing names, open literature

The MARAUDER pattern (a plainly-worded backronym describing exactly what the program does) recurs across the AFWL/Phillips Lab/AFRL Directed Energy lineage. Best-documented examples found:

1. **BEAR — Beam Experiment(s) Aboard a Rocket / Beam Experiment Aboard Rocket.** Neutral particle beam accelerator flown suborbitally from New Mexico, **13 July 1989**, under SDIO sponsorship, technically coordinated by Los Alamos National Laboratory. Primary source: **OSTI 7068110**, G.J. Nunz, *Beam Experiments Aboard a Rocket (BEAR) Project*, LANL report **LA-11737-MS-Vol.1 / BEAR-DT-7-1**, January 1990, sponsor "Department of Defense, Strategic Defense Initiative Organization" (full metadata fetched directly from OSTI). Companion volume at **OSTI 10164015**. Also cataloged at DTIC as **ADA338597**. Abstract states the flight demonstrated NPB accelerator operation and beam propagation in space "as predicted," explicitly framed toward strategic weapons-system development. This is squarely an SDI-era directed-energy program whose title states its content plainly, though the acronym itself is descriptive of method rather than intent-stating like MARAUDER.
2. **CHAMP — Counter-electronics High Power Microwave Advanced Missile Project.** AFRL Directed Energy Directorate (Kirtland AFB) / Boeing. Name states its target and mechanism outright: "counter-electronics," "high power microwave," "advanced missile." First successful flight test publicized **22 October 2012** (Boeing announcement: disabled seven targets before self-destructing). By May 2019 the Air Force had disclosed at least 20 CHAMP-equipped missiles. (Sourced via Wikipedia's CHAMP article, which cites Aviation Week, Boeing, and trade press 2009–2022; I did not independently re-verify each cited article.)
3. **HiJENKS — High-power Joint Electromagnetic Non-Kinetic Strike (weapon).** Named successor to CHAMP; joint ONR/AFRL program. DTIC/NTRL record: **AD1195640** (https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/trecms/pdf/AD1195640.pdf / https://ntrl.ntis.gov/NTRL/dashboard/searchResults/titleDetail/AD1195640.xhtml). Live-fire/performance validation testing completed **August 2022** at Naval Air Warfare Center Weapons Division, China Lake. Name is maximally explicit: "non-kinetic strike" states the weapons intent in the title itself.
4. **RACE — Ring Accelerator Experiment** (LLNL, ~1991) — see §3; direct MARAUDER predecessor, name plainly describes the compact-toroid ring acceleration mechanism.

These four, plus MARAUDER and Shiva Star's original x-ray-simulation role, form a coherent, citable pattern: **DTIC/OSTI is full of directed-energy and pulsed-power hardware whose program names describe, in plain English, exactly what the hardware is meant to do to a target** ("counter-electronics," "non-kinetic strike," "ultra-high directed energy and radiation," "beam experiment... aboard a rocket"), filed as unclassified technical reports and (for the plasma-physics-adjacent ones) published in peer-reviewed venues like *Physics of Fluids B* and *Fusion Technology*.

## 5. What the open literature does NOT establish

This section is required by the task brief and is treated as load-bearing, not boilerplate:

- **A compact toroid accelerated in a 1-meter lab apparatus at Kirtland is not a fielded weapon.** The anchor paper and its OSTI-record descendants (OSTI 121551, etc.) describe laboratory plasma-physics experiments — formation, compression, and acceleration of milligram-scale toroids — not a tested, packaged, or deployed weapon system. Stated *applications* (fast opening switches, x-ray production, RF compression, charge-neutral ion beams, ICF studies — verbatim from the anchor abstract) are a list of research directions the physics *could* support, not evidence any of them were built into a weapon.
- **A stated program goal is not a demonstrated capability.** MARAUDER's abstract goal — "compress toroids to high mass density and magnetic field intensity, and accelerate the toroids to high speed" — is a research objective. Reported 1993 performance figures (mg-scale masses, ~10^8 cm/s target velocities) come from secondary synthesis of the primary paper and were not independently re-verified line-by-line here; even taken at face value they describe a bench apparatus, not a weapon that reached any velocity/mass regime militarily useful against a real target.
- **CHAMP and HiJENKS are the exception, not the rule, and even they are narrowly scoped.** These are the only programs in this set with public evidence of test-range live-fire results against actual electronic targets (2012 CHAMP test; August 2022 HiJENKS testing at China Lake). Even so, open sourcing establishes flight tests of missile-borne HPM payloads against instrumented targets — not doctrine, inventory numbers beyond the reported ~20 CHAMP missiles (itself a 2019 disclosure, not independently re-confirmed here), or confirmed operational deployment in a conflict.
- **The Kugelblitz thread specifically supports no weapon of any kind.** The only concrete output identified from the 1965 Army survey is a *recommendation to define* a research program — there is no evidence in any source reached (primary or secondary) that a ball-lightning weapon was ever built, tested, or even that the recommended follow-on program was funded.
- **Continuity claims are the weakest part of this file.** The MARAUDER-to-FRCHX-to-MTF thread (§3) is a plausible institutional continuity (same building, same capacitor bank, overlapping author names across decades of OSTI records) but is **not** a documented programmatic lineage in the way MARAUDER's own name and abstract are self-documenting. Treat "MARAUDER was absorbed into fusion research" as an inference from circumstantial OSTI-record continuity, not a sourced fact.
- **No document reached in this research states or implies classification of MARAUDER's results.** Claims that "MARAUDER went dark" originate from OSTI-blogger/Substack sources (§3, flagged), not from DTIC, OSTI, or AFRL. This file does not endorse that framing.
