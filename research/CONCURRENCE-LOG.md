## The remark this log exists to test

> *"soon you will see world events in correlation to this report. as we work. watch"*
> — the author, 29 August 2026, recorded **before** any matching event.

That timestamp is the whole point. A correlation claim made *after* the events is
worthless; made *before*, it is testable. This file exists so the claim can be
scored rather than felt.

---

## Why this log is designed the way it is

The report's own §I guardrails apply to the report's own claims about itself, or
they are worth nothing anywhere. Three of them bite directly here:

- **The anti-map.** A shared timeline is not a chain. Two things happening in
  sequence is not one thing causing the other.
- **The guaranteed-null rule.** A claim confirmed by every possible outcome is
  confirmed by none of them.
- **The unfalsifiable machine.** A test that cannot come out against the tester
  is not a test.

A concurrence log without a control is a confirmation engine — it will find
matches forever, because the world produces enough events to match anything.
So the method below is built to be **capable of returning a negative**, and the
author has agreed in advance that a negative gets published.

## Method

### 1. Pre-registration (non-negotiable)
Only an event **dated after** the commit timestamp of the claim it matches can
score. Every entry records the commit SHA and its UTC timestamp. If the commit
cannot be shown to predate the event, the pair is discarded — not argued.

### 2. Specificity gate
The report claim must have been specific enough that **a wrong outcome was
possible**. If no observable event could have contradicted it, it is excluded
as non-diagnostic and logged in the EXCLUDED table with the reason. Vague
structural claims ("power protects itself") never score.

### 3. The 2×2 — all four cells, every time
A hit count alone is meaningless. Each review period reports:

|                          | event occurred | event did not occur |
|--------------------------|----------------|---------------------|
| **report anticipated it**| A (hit)        | B (miss — published)|
| **report did not**       | C (base rate)  | D                   |

**C is the cell that kills the claim.** If comparable events occur constantly
whether or not the report mentioned them, A means nothing. C must be counted
honestly, from the same news sweep, not estimated.

### 4. Misses are published at equal prominence
Cell B — things the report anticipated that did not happen — goes in the same
table, same styling, no softening. A log that only records hits is a horoscope.

### 5. Causation is never claimed
This log measures **concurrence**, and concurrence only. Even a strong A with a
low C establishes that the report was *early*, not that it was *causal*. The
distinction is stated in every entry. Nothing here will ever assert that the
report moved an event in the world.

## Method failure caught in the first sweep — logged because it is the point

**3 September 2026.** While compiling the first review period, a search-summary layer
returned, as a statement of fact, that **the US military had captured Venezuelan President
Nicolás Maduro and his wife in a large-scale attack on the country.**

**It is false.** Checking the dated record entry by entry for August and September 2026
found no such event. What Venezuela has on the record is a 2 August framework with the
Dominican Republic to restore diplomatic relations, and a June earthquake toll now above
6,000. The "capture" traces to **conflict-forecasting literature — scenario planning about
what *might* occur in 2026 — collapsed by a summarising layer into a report of what did.**

**Why this belongs in this log rather than in a footnote.** This log exists to test whether
world events correlate with the report. *A pipeline that silently converts forecasts into
events would manufacture cell-A hits indefinitely* — the correlation engine would be
running on synthetic fuel and every entry after it would be worthless. The claim would have
entered the report as a sourced line and propagated outward.

**Standing rule added, effective immediately:** *no event enters this log, or the report,
from a search summary alone.* It must be confirmed against a dated primary or
primary-adjacent record — and an extraordinary claim about a head of state, a strike, or a
death gets that check **before** it is written down, not after.

**This is also the first published null of the log, and it is a null about the instrument
rather than about the world.** Those are the more valuable kind.
