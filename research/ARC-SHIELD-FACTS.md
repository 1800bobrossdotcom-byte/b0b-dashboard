## Derived arithmetic (mine, from the quoted formulas; not sourced figures)

- Ideal free field, no air absorption, far field: level at r metres = level at 1 m - 20 log10(r). LRAD 2000X continuous 160 dBA at 1 m: 140 at 10 m, 120 at 100 m, 100 at 1,000 m. 450XL continuous 145 dBA: 105 at 100 m; 120 at about 18 m. Real propagation (wind, ground, absorption, the 8 m near field) will differ.
- OSHA Table G-16: 100 dBA = 2 h, 105 = 1 h, 110 = 30 min, 115 = 15 min or less. NIOSH 3-dB rate halves time every +3 dB above 85 (e.g. 100 dBA = 15 min), so NIOSH is stricter at every level above 85.
- NIOSH 1998 double-protection estimate: +5 to 10 dB over the better single device; NIOSH's own limit of usefulness is 105 dBA TWA. Anything measured near the sheets' continuous output (137-160 dBA at 1 m) is far above that, so no consumer earwear is a safe answer at close range; distance is the only large lever and the 10 m manufacturer warning (Edrei opinion) is the sourced standoff figure.

## Corrections to the current guide (`site/tones-shield-guide.html`)

Section 2 (what is LRAD)
1. "Up to 162 dB at 1 meter (LRAD 2000X model)": the 2000X sheet read says 168 dB peak (C-weighted) and 160 dB continuous (A-weighted). 162 not found. State both and the weighting.
2. "Primary Frequency Range: 2,000 - 3,500 Hz (optimized for maximum human discomfort)": not in any manufacturer sheet read; the manufacturer's own brochure says 200 Hz to 10,000 Hz overall. "Optimized for maximum human discomfort" is the guide's gloss, unsourced; the sheets describe "warning and deterrent tones" without a frequency.
3. "Beam Width: 15-30 degrees": sheets say "+/- 15° @ 1kHz/-3dB" i.e. about 30° total at 1 kHz, -3 dB points, and "Variable beam width". Lower frequencies spread wider (Wikipedia).
4. "Effective Range ... pain compliance up to several hundred meters": voice range 5,500 m ideal / 2,000 m over 88 dB noise is sourced (2000X). "Pain compliance ... several hundred meters" is unsourced; the sourced figure is the manufacturer's 10 m danger warning (Edrei opinion). Pain onset "120" is the NYPD report's number as quoted in the opinion ("the 120 decibels threshold where pain begins").
5. "Known deployments against civilians: ... Havana Syndrome incidents ... ": remove Havana from the list. ODNI (2023, 2025) rates a foreign-adversary cause "very unlikely" for most agencies, the NIH found no MRI-detectable injury, and no official source attributes AHIs to LRAD. Ferguson, Standing Rock and Portland are sourced above; Minneapolis 2020 was not checked.
6. "US Patent 6,470,214 (O'Loughlin & Loree, 2002) ... FOIA-confirmed research under Project MEDUSA": the patent is an Air Force speech-via-RF-hearing-effect patent with no MEDUSA link in its text; MEDUSA was a WaveBand / Navy SBIR effort (2003-04), not documented here as FOIA-confirmed, and not DARPA.
7. "Mosquito devices ... Deployed commercially in 35+ countries": NOT VERIFIED; no source read gives a country count.
8. "Documented health effects" lists (PTSD, long-term vestibular injury etc.) and "Infrasonic ... Gavreau" were not checked here; Edrei records alleged auditory pain, migraines, tinnitus, hearing loss, nerve damage (allegations at the pleading stage).

Section 4 (field deployment)
9. 4.1: "~20 dB passive isolation", "ER2 / SE215 provide 35-42 dB": not checked against a spec sheet. NIOSH says "Almost any hearing protector, when fit correctly, can reduce noise by 10 dB" and double protection "will add only 5 to 10 dB"; label-NRR figures should be derated (25/50/70%) or replaced by fit-test wording.
10. 4.2 ("standing wave interference patterns break up the coherent wavefront of an LRAD beam") and 4.4 ("phase cancellation from multiple angles disrupts beam coherence"): nothing read supports or addresses these; the only sourced acoustics are the 6 dB per doubling and the 8 m near field.
