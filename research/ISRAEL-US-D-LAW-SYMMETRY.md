# ISRAEL–US, LINE D: the legal frame and the comparators

Researched 19 Oct 2026. Every quote below was read in spider text or in a PDF extracted locally from the
spider cache (ledger note `israel-us line D`). Search snippets were used only to find URLs. Hashes are
the first 8 hex digits of the ledger sha256. Tiers: documented / attributed / testimony / contested /
unsupported.

**What this line answers.** What "foreign influence" means in US law. Whether the Israel record is unusual
against other states'. What US law actually does to speech about Israel as of Oct 2026. Whether the
Intelligence Community names Israel as a threat.

**Short answers.**
1. Washington's 1796 warning names no country, and it runs both ways: attachment *and* animus. It also
   says the vigilance must be "impartial", or it becomes "the instrument of the very influence to be avoided".
2. In law, "foreign influence" is not a crime. Acting for a foreign principal **without disclosure** is.
   FARA is a disclosure statute, §951 is "espionage lite", and §219 bars public officials from either role.
   None of them names a country. DOJ brought 7 criminal FARA cases in 50 years (1966–2015).
3. The US has prosecuted agents of allies and adversaries alike: Saudi Arabia, the UAE, South Korea,
   Egypt, Qatar, China, Russia, and Israel (Pollard 1986, Franklin 2005, Kadish 2008). Outcomes range from
   conviction to acquittal to a deferred prosecution. The documented Israel cases are of the same
   statutes and the same order of seriousness. They are not an outlier in either direction.
4. **No enacted law or pending bill read here makes posting "conspiracy theories about Israel" unlawful,
   or strips platform immunity for it.** What exists: the IHRA definition used as evidence in Title VI
   enforcement (EO 13899, 2019), EO 14188 (2025), an unpassed Antisemitism Awareness Act, an unpassed
   platform-transparency bill (JASA §6), a nonbinding House resolution, and two §230 repeal bills that are
   content-neutral. The real 2025–26 speech-and-Israel record is **immigration enforcement against
   non-citizens**. A federal court has held that enforcement violated the First Amendment.
5. **Israel is named in none of the unclassified IC threat products read here as an influence or
   espionage threat to the US.** The 2024 and 2025 ATAs name China, Russia and Iran for influence. The 2026
   ATA names China, Russia, Iran and North Korea for cyber and espionage. NCSC 2018 says, without naming
   them, that "U.S. allies or partners often take advantage of the access they enjoy".

## 1. Washington's Farewell Address (1796): documented

**Source.** The primary is *Washington's Farewell Address to the People of the United States*, Senate
Document No. 106–21 (106th Cong., 2nd Sess., 2000), GPO PDF:
`https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CDOC-106sdoc21/pdf/GPO-CDOC-106sdoc21.pdf` (`63c76963`). The
Senate's own copy, same text: `https://www.senate.gov/artandhistory/history/resources/pdf/Washingtons_Farewell_Address.pdf`
(`21b7013e`). Cross-read against Yale Avalon, `https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/washing.asp`
(`3b617fac`). Founders Online refused (see NULLS).

**Date, from the Senate document's introduction:** "It first appeared on September 19, 1796, in the
Philadelphia Daily American Advertiser and then in papers around the country." Also: "Since 1893 the
Senate has observed Washington's birthday by selecting one of its members to read the Farewell Address."

**The passages, verbatim from S. Doc. 106–21, in order.** Avalon differs only in punctuation, e.g. "a
habitual hatred" and "fellow-citizens".

The principle, in the paragraph before:
> "In the execution of such a plan nothing is more essential than that permanent, inveterate antipathies
> against particular nations and passionate attachments for others should be excluded and that in place of
> them just and amicable feelings towards all should be cultivated. The nation which indulges towards another
> an habitual hatred, or an habitual fondness, is in some degree a slave."

The attachment paragraph:
> "So likewise, a passionate attachment of one nation for another produces a variety of evils. Sympathy for
> the favorite nation, facilitating the illusion of an imaginary common interest in cases where no real common
> interest exists and infusing into one the enmities of the other, betrays the former into a participation in
> the quarrels and wars of the latter, without adequate inducement or justification. It leads also to
> concessions to the favorite nation of privileges denied to others … And it gives to ambitious, corrupted,
> or deluded citizens (who devote themselves to the favorite nation) facility to betray or sacrifice the
> interests of their own country without odium, sometimes even with popularity …"

> "As avenues to foreign influence in innumerable ways, such attachments are particularly alarming to the
> truly enlightened and independent patriot. How many opportunities do they afford to tamper with domestic
> factions, to practice the arts of seduction, to mislead public opinion, to influence or awe the public
> councils! Such an attachment of a small or weak towards a great and powerful nation dooms the former to be
> the satellite of the latter."

The vigilance paragraph:
> "Against the insidious wiles of foreign influence (I conjure you to believe me, fellow citizens) the
> jealousy of a free people ought to be constantly awake, since history and experience prove that foreign
> influence is one of the most baneful foes of republican government. But that jealousy to be useful must be
> impartial; else it becomes the instrument of the very influence to be avoided, instead of a defense against
> it. Excessive partiality for one foreign nation and excessive dislike of another cause those whom they
> actuate to see danger only on one side, and serve to veil and even second the arts of influence on the other.
> Real patriots, who may resist the intrigues of the favorite, are liable to become suspected and odious, while
> its tools and dupes usurp the applause and confidence of the people to surrender their interests."

**What the text does and does not do.**
- **These passages name no country.** Elsewhere the address names Great Britain and Spain once, for the
  treaties securing the West, and Europe in "the great rule of conduct". Its own context is "the still
  subsisting war in Europe" and his neutrality proclamation of 22 April 1793. Which nations the 1796
  audience had in mind is not stated in the text and is not asserted here.
- **It runs both ways.** "Antipathies" *and* "attachments"; "habitual hatred" *and* "habitual fondness";
  "excessive partiality" *and* "excessive dislike". Both are named as the same slavery.
- **"Must be impartial"** is the report's symmetry rule in 1796 language. Suspicion that sees "danger only on
  one side" serves "the arts of influence on the other". A reading that quotes only the attachment sentence
  against one state, and drops the animus sentence, is the selective use the passage itself warns against.
- *My reading, labeled:* the "satellite" sentence runs from "a small or weak" nation toward "a great and
  powerful" one. Any use of it should quote it as written.

## 2. The law that governs foreign influence: documented (statute text, Cornell LII)

### 2a. FARA, 22 U.S.C. §611 et seq.
`https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/22/611` (`8f67e419`)
- **Foreign principal (§611(b)(1))** includes "a government of a foreign country and a foreign political
  party". (b)(2) and (b)(3) cover foreign persons and foreign-organised entities.
- **Agent of a foreign principal (§611(c)(1))**: "any person who acts as an agent, representative, employee,
  or servant, or any person who acts in any other capacity at the order, request, or under the direction or
  control, of a foreign principal or of a person any of whose activities are directly or indirectly
  supervised, directed, controlled, financed, or subsidized in whole or in major part by a foreign
  principal". That person must also, in the United States, do one of four things:
  - "engages … in political activities for or in the interests of such foreign principal";
  - acts as PR counsel, publicity agent or political consultant;
  - solicits or disburses money for it; or
  - "represents the interests of such foreign principal before any agency or official of the Government of the United States".
- **Political activities (§611(o))**: "any activity that the person engaging in believes will, or that the person
  intends to, in any way influence any agency or official of the Government of the United States or any section
  of the public within the United States with reference to formulating, adopting, or changing the domestic or
  foreign policies of the United States …"
- **Press exemption (§611(d))**: bona fide news organisations, US-owned at least 80%, and "not owned, directed,
  supervised, controlled, subsidized, or financed" by a foreign principal.

**The LDA exemption, §613(h)** (`https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/22/613`, `6cdb528f`):
> "Any agent of a person described in section 611(b)(2) of this title or an entity described in section
> 611(b)(3) of this title if the agent has engaged in lobbying activities and has registered under the Lobbying
> Disclosure Act of 1995 … in connection with the agent's representation of such person or entity."

*Reading of the text:* the exemption covers agents of foreign **persons and companies** only, under (b)(2) and
(b)(3). An agent of a **foreign government or political party**, under (b)(1), cannot use it, and registers under FARA.

**Penalty (§618(a))** (`94319d32`): willful violation or false statement, "a fine of not more than $10,000 or
… imprisonment for not more than five years, or both".

### 2b. 18 U.S.C. §951, agents of foreign governments
`https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/951` (`4b2ca639`)
> "(a) Whoever, other than a diplomatic or consular officer or attaché, acts in the United States as an agent of
> a foreign government without prior notification to the Attorney General if required in subsection (b), shall
> be fined under this title or imprisoned not more than ten years, or both."
> "(d) … the term 'agent of a foreign government' means an individual who agrees to operate within the United
> States subject to the direction or control of a foreign government or official …"

It excludes accredited diplomats, "officially and publicly acknowledged and sponsored" officials, and "(4) any
person engaged in a legal commercial transaction". §951(e) restores coverage for the agents of Cuba "or any
other country that the President determines … poses a threat".

**DOJ's own gloss (OIG 2016, below):** NSD described §951 as **"espionage lite"**, because such a case
"generally involves espionage-like or clandestine behavior or an otherwise provable connection to an
intelligence service, or information gathering or procurement-type activity". FARA, by contrast, covers
"legal political or quasi-political activities".

### 2c. 18 U.S.C. §219, public officials
`https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/219` (`e2480ad9`): "Whoever, being a public official, is or acts as
an agent of a foreign principal required to register under the Foreign Agents Registration Act of 1938 or a
lobbyist required to register under the Lobbying Disclosure Act of 1995 in connection with the representation
of a foreign entity … shall be fined under this title or imprisoned for not more than two years, or both."
This is the statute in the Menendez (Egypt) conviction.

### 2d. Lobbying Disclosure Act, 2 U.S.C. §1601 et seq.
- **Lobbyist (§1602(10))** (`e49bcf37`): "any individual who is employed or retained by a client for financial or
  other compensation for services that include more than one lobbying contact", unless lobbying is under 20% of
  their time for that client over 3 months.
- **Foreign-entity disclosure (§1603(b)(4))** (`b9476fe9`): registrants must disclose any foreign entity that
  "holds at least 20 percent equitable ownership in the client" or "directly or indirectly, in whole or in major
  part, plans, supervises, controls, directs, finances, or subsidizes the activities of the client".

**The point that travels:** none of these statutes names a country, and none criminalises advocacy as such. The
offence is acting under a foreign principal's direction or control without the required disclosure. Domestic
advocacy for a foreign state's interests, without direction or control, falls under none of them.

### 2e. Enforcement record: documented
**DOJ OIG, *Audit of the National Security Division's Enforcement and Administration of the Foreign Agents
Registration Act*, Audit Division 16-24, September 2016**: `https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/oig-reports/a1624.pdf`
(`7c182f7f`, extracted locally). Findings, verbatim:
- "the number of active FARA registrations peaked in the 1980s, with a high of 916 active registrations in 1987,
  and began to fall sharply in the mid-1990s". NSD speculated that the 1993 fees and the 1995 LDA, "which carved
  out a significant exemption to FARA", were likely factors.
- "Between 1966 and 2015 the Department only brought seven criminal FARA cases – one resulted in a conviction at
  trial for conspiracy to violate FARA and other statutes, two pleaded guilty to violating FARA, two others pleaded
  guilty to non-FARA charges, and the remaining two cases were dismissed."
- "the Department has not sought civil injunctive relief under FARA since 1991".
- "62 percent of initial registrations were untimely, and … 50 percent of registrants filed at least one
  supplemental statement late".
- The FARA Unit "has issued approximately 130 letters of inquiry over the past ten years. Thirty-eight of the
  recipients were found to have an obligation to register".
- NSD named think tanks, NGOs, campus groups, foreign media and grassroots organisations "that may receive funding
  and direction from foreign governments" as the hard exemption cases; such groups "generally claim that they act
  independently of foreign control".
- NSD argued that those lobbying for foreign *commercial* interests "should not be exempt from FARA registration".
- 14 recommendations.

**The February 2025 narrowing: documented.** AG memorandum "General Policy Regarding Charging, Plea Negotiations,
and Sentencing", 5 Feb 2025: `https://www.justice.gov/ag/media/1388541/dl` (`c1172b65`):
> "Shifting Resources in the National Security Division. To free resources to address more pressing priorities,
> and end risks of further weaponization and abuses of prosecutorial discretion, the Foreign Influence Task Force
> shall be disbanded. Recourse to criminal charges under the Foreign Agents Registration Act (FARA) and 18 U.S.C.
> § 951 shall be limited to instances of alleged conduct similar to more traditional espionage by foreign
> government actors. With respect to FARA and § 951, the Counterintelligence and Export Control Section, including
> the FARA Unit, shall focus on civil enforcement, regulatory initiatives, and public guidance."

*(The same-day memo `1388546`, "Total Elimination of Cartels…", was also read. It carries no FARA language and is
not the source.)*

**2025 enforcement in practice: attributed.** Mayer Brown, 12 Jan 2026, a law-firm analysis
(`a13b6d60`): "FARA enforcement pivoted in 2025, but it did not halt."
- Most pending cases continued.
- The §219 counts against a sitting congressman (Azerbaijan, Mexico) were dismissed in July 2025, citing the memo.
- In the South Korea case, "DOJ steadfastly maintained that FARA is appropriately applied to activities on behalf
  of any undisclosed principal, not merely foreign adversary nations."
- **That last item is DOJ's position as the firm reports it. The filings were not read.**

- Outcomes vary: conviction, acquittal, deferred prosecution, dismissal, and a fine-only sentence (Kadish).
- **The documented Israel record is three cases (1986, 2006, 2009), all ending in pleas.** None was found from
  2015–2026. *That is a search-level null:* DOJ's NSD case index refused (403), and search is not exhaustive.

  protection either. The instrument records charges, not conduct; the same rule as XVI's wanted-list entry.

- **Since Feb 2025, criminal FARA/§951 charges are policy-limited to espionage-like conduct for every country
  alike** (§2e). Fewer influence cases from here on is a policy fact, not a country fact.

## 4. Speech about Israel and US law, 2024–2026

**Overlap note.** Line A (`research/ISRAEL-US-A-CURRENT.md` §1) already traces the viral "Section 230 … target
Americans … conspiracy theories about Israel" post. It goes back to a 17 Sept 2025 oversight-hearing exchange.
Line A also covers the §230 text and both repeal bills (S. 3546, H.R. 10332). This section does not repeat
that. It adds the rest of the legal frame.

### 4a. The IHRA working definition: core text documented, examples attributed
- **The core definition** is quoted in a US primary, **EO 13899, "Combating Anti-Semitism", 11 Dec 2019**
  (84 FR 68779; `https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2019-12-16/html/2019-27217.htm`, `b7e1762d`):
  > "the non-legally binding working definition of anti-Semitism adopted on May 26, 2016, by the International
  > Holocaust Remembrance Alliance (IHRA), which states, 'Antisemitism is a certain perception of Jews, which may
  > be expressed as hatred toward Jews. Rhetorical and physical manifestations of antisemitism are directed toward
  > Jewish or non-Jewish individuals and/or their property, toward Jewish community institutions and religious
  > facilities'".
- Agencies "shall consider" this, and the IHRA "Contemporary Examples … to the extent that any examples might be
  useful as evidence of discriminatory intent".
- The EO's limit, §2(b): "agencies shall not diminish or infringe upon any right protected under Federal law or
  under the First Amendment".
- **The Israel-related examples are attributed**, read in the Wikipedia copy (`9db1e003`). IHRA's own site, AJC,
  State's archived page and three government copies all refused or were decoys (see NULLS).
- The caveat sentence that travels with them:
  > "Manifestations might include the targeting of the state of Israel, conceived as a Jewish collectivity.
  > However, criticism of Israel similar to that leveled against any other country cannot be regarded as antisemitic."
- The examples are "taking into account the overall context". Seven of the eleven mention Israel:
  - "Accusing the Jews as a people, or Israel as a state, of inventing or exaggerating the Holocaust";
  - "[a]ccusing Jewish citizens of being more loyal to Israel, or to the alleged priorities of Jews worldwide,
    than to the interests of their own nations";
  - "[d]enying the Jewish people their right to self-determination, e.g., by claiming that the existence of a
    State of Israel is a racist endeavor";
  - "[a]pplying double standards by requiring of [Israel] a behavior not expected or demanded of any other
    democratic nation";
  - "[u]sing the symbols and images associated with classic antisemitism (e.g., claims of Jews killing Jesus or
    blood libel) to characterize Israel or Israelis";
  - "[d]rawing comparisons of contemporary Israeli policy to that of the Nazis";
  - "[h]olding Jews collectively responsible for actions of the state of Israel."
- **Contested** (attributed, Wikipedia). Kenneth Stern "coordinated the drafting of the original working definition
  and its examples" (2003–04, at the AJC). He says both things:
  - in 2015, it "remains a very good one";
  - against campus or legal adoption, "It was never intended to be a campus hate speech code", and legal status
    would be "unconstitutional and unwise".
- *Relevance to the report's trope guard:* the "dual loyalty" and "collective responsibility" examples are the
  conflation the brief already forbids, namely Israel's government ≠ Jews or Jewish Americans.

### 4b. Antisemitism Awareness Act: documented (govinfo bill status XML)

### 4c. Executive Order 14188, "Additional Measures To Combat Anti-Semitism": documented
Signed 29 Jan 2025; published 90 FR 8847, 3 Feb 2025 (`https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-02-03/html/2025-02230.htm`, `432e0712`).
- §2: "It shall be the policy of the United States to combat anti-Semitism vigorously, using all available and
  appropriate legal tools, to prosecute, remove, or otherwise hold to account the perpetrators of unlawful
  anti-Semitic harassment and violence."
- §3(c): "The Attorney General is encouraged to employ appropriate civil-rights enforcement authorities, such as
  18 U.S.C. 241".
- §3(e): State, Education and DHS to recommend "familiarizing institutions of higher education with the grounds
  for inadmissibility under 8 U.S.C. 1182(a)(3) so that such institutions may monitor for and report activities by
  alien students and staff relevant to those grounds and for ensuring that such reports about aliens lead … to
  investigations and, if warranted, actions to remove such aliens."
- **The order does not mention IHRA, social media or platforms.** Its operative edge is "unlawful" harassment and
  violence, plus immigration grounds for **non-citizens**. It "reaffirms Executive Order 13899".

### 4d. Jewish American Security Act: documented (bill text and status)
- **S. 4576**, introduced 19 May 2026 by Rosen, with Lankford, Schumer, Risch, Fetterman, Young, Slotkin and
  Schatz. Referred to HSGAC; **no further action** (`ecb7f292`, status `57af801d`).
- **House companion H.R. 9211**, introduced 9 Jun 2026; **in subcommittees since 7 Jul 2026** (`136395fa`).
- **§6 "Documenting Online Antisemitism"** is the only platform provision. It is a **transparency mandate on
  platforms**, not a rule about users. Platforms with "at least 50,000,000 unique monthly users in the United
  States" would every 180 days publish:
  - their moderation practices;
  - the amount of content "determined to be violative by the online platform" as antisemitic;
  - their actions taken;
  - recommendation-algorithm amplification;
  - accounts removed, including bot and foreign-terrorist-linked accounts.

  Enforcement is by the FTC, as an unfair or deceptive practice.
- **"Antisemitic content" is whatever the platform's own policies say. The bill supplies no definition for §6.**
- **It does not amend §230.** "230" and "interactive computer service" do not occur in it.
- Its findings say antisemitism includes Jews being "held responsible for the policies of the Israeli government".
  *Flag:* finding (21) dates EO 14188 to "January 30, 2025". The Federal Register says 29 Jan 2025.

### 4f. §230 and the First Amendment limit: documented
- **§230(c)(1):** "No provider or user of an interactive computer service shall be treated as the publisher or
  speaker of any information provided by another information content provider."
- **§230(e)(1):** nothing in it impairs "any other Federal criminal statute". Source: Cornell `af692804`; the last
  amendment listed is 2018. The OLRC copy was a 200 "Page Not Found".
- §230 never shielded the poster, and repealing it creates no offence. Line A §1c covers this.
- **The constitutional floor.** *Brandenburg v. Ohio*, 395 U.S. 444 (1969), per curiam (Cornell, `40c464ee`): the
  First Amendment does "not permit a State to forbid or proscribe advocacy of the use of force or of law violation
  except where such advocacy is directed to inciting or producing imminent lawless action and is likely to incite or
  produce such action".
- *Matal v. Tam*, No. 15-1293, decided 19 June 2017, **plurality** part of the opinion (Alito, J., Part IV)
  (slip opinion `https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/16pdf/15-1293_1o13.pdf`, `a265672c`): "Speech that demeans
  on the basis of race, ethnicity, gender, religion, age, disability, or any other similar ground is hateful; but the
  proudest boast of our free speech jurisprudence is that we protect the freedom to express 'the thought that we hate.'"
- *Reading, labeled:* a statute making a viewpoint about a foreign state ("conspiracy theories about Israel")
  unlawful would face this floor. Speech outside it (true threats, incitement, defamation of a person, harassment
  as conduct) is reachable whichever country it concerns.

### 4h. The question asked, answered
**Does any enacted law or pending bill, as of 19 Oct 2026, make posting "conspiracy theories about Israel"
