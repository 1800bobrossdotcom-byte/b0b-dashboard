# Does the US arm Sudan through the UAE, and are US weapons in these wars?

**21 September 2026. Research, unpublished.** Companion to `research/SUDAN-ARMS.md`. Sources in
`research/sources/ledger.jsonl`; refusals recorded.

Two questions from the author, answered separately because they have different answers:
1. Does the US arm Sudan *via* arming the UAE?
2. Are US weapons in "those wars" — Sudan, and the UAE's other wars, Yemen and Libya?

---

## 1. The legal frame — documented, statute read at source

**22 U.S.C. § 2753 (Arms Export Control Act, ledgered).** A country buying US defense articles
must agree "not to transfer title to, or possession of, any defense article … to anyone not an
officer, employee, or agent of that country … and not to use or permit the use of such article …
for purposes other than those for which furnished **unless the consent of the President has first
been obtained**" (§2753(a)(2)). The President may not consent to a retransfer "unless the United
States itself would transfer the defense article under consideration to that country"
(§2753(a)). A country that uses articles "in substantial violation" of those agreements becomes
ineligible for credits, and "no cash sales or deliveries pursuant to previous sales may be made"
(§2753(c)) — the cut-off exists in the statute and is discretionary in practice.

**What this means for the question.** Sudan is under a UN arms embargo (Darfur, res. 1591) and
under US sanctions; the United States would not itself transfer a defense article to the RSF.
So any US-origin article the UAE passed to the RSF would be an unauthorised retransfer under
§2753(a)(2), and the law's own remedy is §2753(c). Whether that has happened is the evidence
question below.

## 2. Q1: US-origin weapons in Sudan via the UAE — the evidence

**Documented: nothing.** No primary document read here — not the two OFAC designations, not
the ICJ order, not the Security Council Report summary of the Panel of Experts — identifies a
US-origin weapon in RSF or SAF hands. Treasury's RSF designations name a UAE-based company
that "provided the RSF with money and military equipment" without naming the equipment's origin.

**Attributed, and the null is the finding.** The secondary record's third-party supplier list
for the RSF is **Canada** (Streit Group armoured vehicles — a UN Panel accusation in 2016 over
24 vehicles sold in 2012; Sterling Cross rifles), **Israel** (LAR-160 rocket mounts and Galil ACE
carbines found in 2023, "likely purchased and transferred by a third party"), **Greece** (a 2022
licence for Cytrox's Predator spyware on a Cyprus–Athens–Khartoum flight, admitted by a deputy
minister in 2023), and the **United Kingdom** (Sudan's October 2025 submission to the Security
Council: Militec small-arms target devices and Cummins UK engines in Emirati-built Nimr APCs,
"sold by UK firms to the UAE, which then sent them to the RSF"; both companies deny). Amnesty's
weapons tracing (2024, 2025) names China, Russia, Turkey, Yemen, the UAE and Serbia as origins.
**The United States is on none of these lists.** Chinese-made Norinco howitzers and guided bombs
are the items Amnesty traces *through* the UAE, not American ones.

**So the honest answer to "does the US arm Sudan via the UAE" is: not on any document or
tracing report found, and the people looking hardest — Amnesty, the UN Panel, Bellingcat,
Sudan's own government filing to the Security Council — have named Canadian, British, Israeli,
Chinese, Serbian and Emirati-built kit, and not American.** Guaranteed-null caution applies in
one direction only: the absence of US-origin kit in the tracing record is evidence about what
has been found, and the tracers are motivated and competent. It is weak evidence that none is
there; it is strong evidence that if any is, it is not what the RSF is fighting with.

**What the US *does* do, documented and attributed.** It keeps arming the UAE while the UAE
arms the RSF, and it has said so in its own voice:
- **GAO-22-105988 (June 2022, read at source):** DOD "administered at least $54.6 billion of
  military support to Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates from fiscal years 2015 through
  2021," mostly Foreign Military Sales — "helicopters, missiles, and small diameter bombs."
- Biden designated the UAE a **"major defense partner"** on 23 Sept 2024, three days after five
  House members wrote to the White House about the UAE's role in Sudan (attributed).
- **March 2025:** Rep. Gregory Meeks placed a hold on arms sales to the UAE over Sudan
  (Politico, attributed). **May 2025:** State approved **$1.32bn of CH-47F Chinooks and $130m of
  F-16 support** to the UAE ahead of the President's Abu Dhabi visit (The Hill, attributed).
  **June 2025:** Senate joint resolutions of disapproval were introduced against the sales, with
  more than thirty NGOs including Amnesty backing them (attributed); **their outcome was not
  verified here.** **November 2025:** Secretary Rubio "called for international action to stop the
  flow of weapons to the RSF" and said the US "knows which countries are involved in supplying the
  RSF and using their territories for transit" (BBC, attributed).
- **June 2024:** OFAC placed seven UAE-based companies under "blocked pending investigation" for
  Sudan sanctions violations (attributed; Treasury release not fetched).

That is the shape: the retransfer statute would bite on US kit; the US has not found US kit; the
US has cut nothing off and has expanded the relationship, while its own Secretary of State says
it knows who the suppliers are. The report's framing for this is *the withholding proves the
conflict of interest, not the contents* — the US is a party with an interest in not finding.

## 3. Q2: Are US weapons in the UAE's other wars? — Yemen: documented. Libya: attributed.

**Yemen — the strongest record, and it is the precedent for Sudan.**
- **Amnesty International, 6 Feb 2019 (read at source, ledgered):** "A wide variety of
  US-supplied armoured vehicles equipped with heavy machine guns, including **M-ATV, Caiman and
  MaxxPro** models, have been documented in the hands of UAE-backed militias Security Belt,
  Shabwani elite forces and 'The Giants'." Western states had supplied the UAE with at least
  $3.5bn of arms since March 2015; suppliers named include the USA.
- **CNN, Feb 2019 (read at source, ledgered):** US-made **Oshkosh** armoured vehicles with the
  Abu Abbas brigade (its founder US-designated a terrorist in 2017); **MaxxPro MRAPs** with the
  UAE-backed Giants Brigade; American **TOW** missiles airdropped by the Saudis onto a front
  where AQAP fought. "Recipients of US weaponry are legally obligated to adhere to end-use
  requirements which prohibit the transferring of any equipment to third parties without prior
  authorization … **That authorization was never obtained.**" Pentagon spokesman, on record: "The
  United States has not authorized the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia or the United Arab Emirates to
  re-transfer any equipment to parties inside Yemen." A senior UAE official: the militia was
  under UAE supervision, so the kit was in the coalition's "collective possession."
- **GAO, June 2022 (read at source):** "despite several reports that airstrikes and other attacks
  by Saudi Arabia and UAE have caused extensive civilian harm in Yemen, **DOD has not reported
  and State could not provide evidence that it investigated any incidents of potential
  unauthorized use** of equipment transferred to Saudi Arabia or UAE." DOD officials "lack
  guidance for reporting such incidents, and State officials could not provide specific
  guidance." State filed one 2018 certification and "did not submit two subsequent, required
  certifications."

- **Direct use, not retransfer — the second branch, supplied by the author (Columbia Magazine,
  Fall 2022, read at source, ledgered).** A joint investigation by the **Security Force Monitor**
  (Columbia Law School Human Rights Institute) and the **Washington Post**, published 4 June 2022
  with a companion essay in *Just Security* by Tony Wilson and Priyanka Motaparthy, "concluded
  that a 'substantial portion' of airstrikes that have killed civilians in Yemen have been carried
  out by jets developed, sold, and maintained by US companies and flown by US-trained pilots."
  Method: thousands of public news items, photographs and videos matched against US military
  contracts to show that particular **Saudi, Emirati, Kuwaiti and Bahraini fighter squadrons**
  flying over Yemen "are heavily reliant on US equipment and training." The authors' own line:
  "It is high time for the United States to finally spend the time and resources to review
  whether coalition squadrons that benefitted from U.S. military contracts conducted attacks in
  Yemen that may constitute war crimes — and to disclose their findings to Congress and the
  public." Against the government position the article quotes — that "there is no reason to
  believe that American-made arms are commonly used in such strikes" — and against GAO's finding
  the same month that nobody had investigated. **Tier: the investigation's existence, authorship,
  date and headline finding are documented from the Columbia page; the squadron-level data are
  attributed** — the *Just Security* URL answered the crawler with a redirect to the article's
  header image (HTTP 200, `image/jpeg`; a new decoy shape, recorded), the Monitor's own post is
  404, and the Post is robots-disallowed.

So in Yemen the record has two branches. The UAE retransferred US-origin armoured vehicles to
militias without consent, the Pentagon confirmed no consent was given, and the executive
branch's own auditor found nobody investigated. And the coalition's own airstrikes on civilians
were flown, in substantial part, in US-built and US-maintained jets by US-trained crews. **That is the documented precedent for the question the author is asking about
Sudan: the mechanism exists, has been used, and was not policed.**

**Libya — attributed, one strong specimen.** In June 2019 the Tripoli government found a cache
of **US-made Javelin anti-tank missiles** at a captured Haftar base south of Tripoli; shipping
markings showed them originally sold to Oman and the UAE in 2008 (NYT; the article refused the
crawler, so attributed via the secondary record). The UN reported the UAE supplied Haftar with
aircraft, over 100 armoured vehicles and over $200m (attributed). Haftar's LNA is, in turn, one
of the documented conduits to the RSF (`SUDAN-ARMS.md`).

**Sudan — no US-origin kit found (§2).** The three wars line up as: Yemen, documented; Libya,
attributed with a physical specimen; Sudan, null on the tracing record so far.

## 3b. Israeli systems entering the same way — the parallel test

The author's third question: do Israeli weapons systems enter these wars by the same route? The
mechanism is structurally identical — Israel licenses exports under the **Defense Export Control
Law 5767-2007** (DECA), with end-user undertakings that stand where the AECA's §2753(a)(2)
consent rule stands — and the record, tiered:

**Sudan — attributed, two physical specimens and one spyware flight.**
- **Israeli-made LAR-160 light rocket-artillery mounts and Galil ACE 31 carbines** were
  documented in RSF hands in August and October 2023. The secondary record's own caveat: "likely
  purchased and transferred to Sudan by a third party, or possibly through international arms
  trafficking." No source names the third party; the UAE is the obvious candidate and is not
  documented as the conduit for these items.
- **Predator spyware (Cytrox/Intellexa, Tal Dilian — Israeli subsidiaries Balinese Ltd and
  Peterbald Ltd)** reached Khartoum in **2022** on a Cessna from Cyprus via Athens carrying three
  undeclared surveillance systems; a Greek deputy foreign minister admitted in 2023 to licensing
  the export while denying it fed the war; Predator is reported sold to the RSF. **OFAC designated
  Intellexa-consortium individuals and entities in March 2024.** This one is a *documented* chain
  end to end except for the last step (who in Sudan received it), which is attributed to the RSF.
- **Israel's own posture toward the belligerents is documented in outline:** Netanyahu met
  Burhan in Uganda in February 2020; a foreign-minister visit to Khartoum on 2 Feb 2023; and on
  16 April 2023, the day after the war began, Israel announced it was mediating "through the
  Mossad and the Israeli Foreign Ministry," **"in contact with the two leaders"** — Burhan and
  Hemedti. Contact with both sides is documented; supply to either is not.

**Libya — attributed, and the specimen is Emirati-mediated.** In **April 2020** the UAE deployed a
"secretly-purchased, advanced, Israeli-made" air-defence system through Haftar's LNA, reported as
a counter to Turkish drones (The New Arab; the Second Libyan Civil War record). Earlier reporting
(2017–2018) has Haftar receiving Israeli military aid "following UAE-mediated meetings with
Mossad agents"; the LNA denies. **This is the cleanest Israeli instance of the pattern the
author asked about: a system bought by the UAE and passed to a third-party force in an
embargoed war**, exactly as the US-made Javelins found at a Haftar base in 2019 were sold to
the UAE in 2008. Attributed, not documented — no end-user certificate or licence has surfaced.

**Yemen — nothing found.** Amnesty's 2019 list of the UAE's Western suppliers does not include
Israel; Israel–UAE relations were unofficial before August 2020. Reports of Israel–UAE "spy
bases" on Socotra (Aug 2020) are intelligence infrastructure, not weapons, and attributed.

**The structural point, which is the report's.** Post-Abraham Accords, Israel's defence exports
roughly doubled in five years to nearly **$15bn in 2024** (SIPRI has Israel seventh worldwide,
2021–2025), "including to nations that publicly avoid such deals with Israel." Every one of
those sales carries a DECA end-user undertaking. **The page has zero mentions of DECA** (noted in
the operating memory as the real gap in the Israel line). The Sudan and Libya specimens are the
same finding the US line produced: **the licence is the document; the retransfer is the
mechanism; the absence of enforcement is the finding.** For Israel the equivalent of GAO-22-105988
— an official audit of end-use monitoring — does not exist in the public record found here, and
that null is itself the difference between the two systems.

**Limits.** The LAR-160/Galil sightings, the Predator flight and the Libyan air-defence system
were not read at their primary sources (Ynet/Haaretz, the Greek parliamentary record, The New
Arab) — all attributed. Cytrox's Israeli subsidiaries are corporate-registry facts carried by the
secondary record.

## 4. Symmetry and limits

- The Yemen and Libya findings run against the UAE and, in Yemen, against the US end-use
  system. Printed at full weight. The Sudan null runs the other way and is printed at the
  same weight: no one has produced an American weapon from the Sudan war.
- Iran, not the US, is the documented external drone supplier to the SAF (OFAC, 16 Jan 2025).
- The June 2025 Senate resolutions' fate, the DSCA notification texts (dsca.mil 403), the
  Politico and Hill pieces, and the NYT Javelin story were not read at source; each is marked
  attributed. The DOD Inspector General's own end-use report (dodig.mil 403) would sit beside
  the GAO's and was not obtained.
- Nothing here bears on the living-person floor: the actors are states, companies and militias.
