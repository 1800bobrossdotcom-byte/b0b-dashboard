### 1c. What Section 230 does: documented
47 U.S.C. §230 at `https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/230` (`af692804…`). The last amendment it lists is Pub. L. 115-164, 11 Apr 2018.
- §230(c)(1): "No provider or user of an interactive computer service shall be treated as the publisher or speaker of any information provided by another information content provider."
- §230(c)(2) shields "any action voluntarily taken in good faith to restrict access to or availability of material that the provider or user considers to be obscene, lewd, lascivious, filthy, excessively violent, harassing, or otherwise objectionable".
- §230(e)(1): "Nothing in this section shall be construed to impair the enforcement of … any other Federal criminal statute."

**In two sentences:** §230 stops platforms and users from being held civilly liable as the publisher of what *someone else* posted, and protects good-faith moderation. It never shielded the person who wrote a post, and it does not touch federal criminal law.

*My reading of the text, not a holding:*
- Repealing or "reforming" 230 creates no government power to act against people for their posts.
- A poster's own liability is unchanged either way.
- The realistic effect on posters is indirect: platforms facing civil suits may remove more user content. That is the general collateral-censorship argument, which civil-liberties critics make about 230 repeal in general.
- That argument is content-neutral. Nothing read here makes it Israel-specific.

**FBI role:** none in enacting it. U.S. Const. art. I, §1: "All legislative powers herein granted shall be vested in a Congress of the United States…" (`https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/articlei`, `460ffcd7…`). An FBI Director's "I will work with you" is a statement of support. It is not an instrument.

## 3. "War criminal" at tier: the ICC warrant since 21 Nov 2024

**Tier statement:**
- What exists is an **arrest warrant**: Pre-Trial Chamber I's finding of reasonable grounds under art. 58, which the report already carries.
- There has been no confirmation of charges, no trial and no conviction. "War criminal" as a noun describes no judicial finding at any tier.
- The warrant is **in force**, and its jurisdictional basis is **contested and still pending**.

### 3a. Israel's challenges: documented from ICC records read in full
- **ICC-01/18-468, PTC I, 17 Oct 2025.** Bench: Guillou (presiding), Alapini-Gansou, Hohler. URL `https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/CourtRecords/0902ebd180ccfb3b.pdf` (`2d6a070a…`, extracted locally). Its procedural history:
  - **23 Sept 2024:** Israel files its art. 19(2) jurisdiction challenge.
  - **21 Nov 2024:** "the Chamber rejected Israel's Jurisdiction Challenge as premature … On the same day, the Chamber issued three warrants of arrest".
  - **27 Nov 2024:** notice of appeal. **13 Dec 2024:** appeal brief.
  - **24 Apr 2025:** "The Appeals Chamber reversed the Article 19(2) Decision for insufficient reasoning and remanded the matter to the Chamber for it to rule on the substance of the jurisdictional challenge. The Appeals Chamber dismissed Israel's request for suspensive effect of the Warrants as moot."
  - **9 May 2025:** Israel asks for the warrants to be withdrawn.
  - **16 July 2025:** rejected.
  - **22 July 2025:** leave to appeal sought.
  - **17 Oct 2025:** "THE CHAMBER HEREBY REJECTS the Request."
- **ICC-01/18-457, PTC I, 16 July 2025** (`0902ebd180c1587a.pdf`, `f0b85b83…`):
  - ¶17: "Pursuant to article 58(4) of the Statute, once issued, warrants of arrest shall remain in effect until decided otherwise by the Court."
  - ¶25: "Israel's Jurisdiction Challenge is thus currently pending before the Chamber. It is inherent in the nature of jurisdictional challenges that they may result in the overturning of a prior ascertainment of jurisdiction. - **15 Dec 2025, Appeals Chamber, art. 18(1):** "by majority, confirmed Pre-Trial Chamber I's decision rejecting Israel's request for an order requiring the Prosecutor to issue a new notification under Article 18(1)".
