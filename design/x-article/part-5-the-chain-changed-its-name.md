In 1833 Britain abolished slavery and paid £20 million in compensation. It went to the slaveholders, for their "loss of property." The enslaved received nothing.

The money was borrowed. The Treasury redeemed the associated perpetual bonds in 2015, according to its own 2018 FOI response. The debt had been refinanced into ordinary government debt across the 1800s, so it was never one 182-year loan. But "the public was still paying down slaveholder-compensation debt into the 21st century."

Hold that shape: an ending on paper, and the transaction underneath it carrying on.

## The exception

The 13th Amendment ended slavery "except as a punishment for crime".

The Southern states filled the exception at once. The Black Codes made unemployment, loitering and vagrancy crimes, and the people convicted under them were leased to mines, railroads and farms. Convict leasing ran from 1865 to 1928.

The exception is still in force. Prison wages average $0.13 to $0.52 an hour, and six states pay nothing. Federal Prison Industries, UNICOR, took in $531 million in FY2022.

## What the camps built

The Gulag was an economy as much as a prison system. Norilsk was built by the prisoners of Norillag, roughly 500,000 of them, with an estimated 17,000–18,000 deaths. Nornickel there is now the world's largest producer of palladium and refined nickel. The report's line: "The camps closed. The extraction continued."

The ILO counts 27.6 million people in forced labour, 3.9 million of them in labour imposed by states. Its 2024 update puts the profit at $236 billion a year, up roughly 57% from the 2014 estimate.

**Here is a popular version the report corrects.** The story usually puts the cobalt in your phone in eastern Congo's war. The DRC does mine about three-quarters of the world's cobalt, but in the southern Copperbelt, "not the eastern conflict zone." Amnesty International found children as young as seven in its artisanal mines. The extraction is real, and the claim only holds if its location is right.

## What repeats

Section XIV asks whether this is the first cycle or one of many. Its strongest evidence is the libraries. Alexandria was destroyed in stages across 700 years. Baghdad's House of Wisdom went in 1258, and Nalanda burned in 1193. In 1562 Bishop Diego de Landa ordered thousands of Maya manuscripts burned, and four codices survive. The report's reading: "The knowledge asymmetry is the control mechanism."

The same section kills more than it keeps:

- **Göbekli Tepe was not deliberately buried.** Its excavators now attribute the fill to slope slides: "a review of the available evidence now suggests an inundation by displaced archaeological deposits from adjacent higher-lying knolls and slopes."
- **A comet causing the Younger Dryas is contested.** The cooling is documented. Greenland's platinum spike came "too late to have triggered the cooling", and PLOS One has retracted two of the Comet Research Group's papers.
- **The leap to "one literal global memory" behind the flood stories is unsupported.**

The Olympics show the pattern in public. Mexico City's Games opened ten days after the army fired on student protesters at Tlatelolco. The government admitted 4 deaths; the estimates run to 300–400.

## The map beneath the map

Section XVI reads the wars through what runs underneath them. In Sudan the report sorts the evidence into two layers.

**The documented layer.** On 7 January 2025 the US Treasury designated the RSF's commander. In the same action it designated a UAE-based holding company that "has provided the RSF with money and military equipment". It also designated a UAE-based gold buyer that "has purchased gold from Sudan, presumably for the benefit of the RSF, and subsequently transported it to Dubai." Every company Treasury names in the RSF's procurement network is UAE-based.

The tier line comes with it: "Treasury does not say the Emirati state supplied the weapons; it says UAE-based companies did."

**The attributed layer.** Emirati cargo flights through Amdjarass in Chad are reported by four named outlets, and the UN Panel of Experts called the accusations "credible". The Panel's report itself was not obtained, so this stays attributed.

**Killed in both directions:** "The ICJ cleared the UAE" and "the ICJ heard the evidence" are both false. The Court removed Sudan's case for manifest lack of jurisdiction, which is no ruling on the merits.

**The enforcement gap.** Under US law, a buyer of American weapons may not pass them on without the President's consent. The Government Accountability Office counted "at least $54.6 billion of military support to Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates from fiscal years 2015 through 2021". It found that "DOD has not reported and State could not provide evidence that it investigated any incidents of potential unauthorized use". No equivalent public end-use audit for Israel was found.

On 21 September 2026 Britain agreed to refuel Saudi aircraft in the Yemen war. That is the support the United States and the Saudi-led coalition ended in November 2018 over the war's civilian casualties. On 9 October 2026 the Treasury designated the International Criminal Court itself. The order's stated reason is the Court's arrest warrants for Israel's prime minister and former defence minister. Those warrants rest on "reasonable grounds to believe", below the bar for confirming charges. The same Chamber "could not determine that all elements of the crime against humanity of extermination were met."

**One more killed specimen.** On Nord Stream, "the sabotage is fact; who ordered it is unproven". The Hersh account rests on a single anonymous source, and the Russia-did-it theory is contradicted by Russia owning the pipeline.
